What this page can and cannot show

The consultation never asked respondents to distinguish peer-reviewed research from grey literature, so no question on this page separates a journal article from a trade-association white paper. Three independent signals are assembled instead, and each one is weaker than it looks:

  • Self-declared basis (Q138, Q56, Q122). Respondents chose how they knew what they claimed. Nothing was verified, and Q138 allowed several answers at once.
  • Citation mining of free text. A "citation signal" is a string match — a URL, a DOI, "et al.", "peer-review", ".pdf", a journal name. It shows that a source was named, not that it was read, relevant, or supportive of the point being made.
  • Template detection. Identical long passages recur across respondents, citations and all, so an unduplicated count of "respondents citing X" overstates how many people independently went looking.

Counts measure mobilisation, not truth: a consultation is not a referendum, and a well-sourced minority is still a minority. Every panel below states its own base and what it cannot tell you.
