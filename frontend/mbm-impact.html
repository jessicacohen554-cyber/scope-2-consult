Impact in the Market-Based Method

Should the scope 2 market-based method drive real emissions reductions, and does the proposal go far enough?

Two different things called "impact"

1 · System impact in the MBM

The market-based method itself should push the grid to cleaner supply, not just reallocate existing clean power. People call it additionality, incrementality, causal impact, newness or asset age, or market scarcity. This page is mainly about this.

2 · Consequential or marginal accounting

A different method: estimate avoided emissions against a counterfactual using marginal emission rates. Respondents disagree on whether it should replace the attributional MBM, sit beside it inside scope 2, or live outside it.

Does the proposal go far enough on impact?

Everyone who took a position on whether hourly matching + deliverability + SSS, as proposed, delivers real impact.

Not far enough = right direction, needs more (e.g. an incrementality test). Different approach = the proposal undermines impact; wants e.g. annual matching plus additionality, or emissionality. Too far = impact is not the MBM's job.

How this was measured
  • Who was read: everyone whose free text uses an umbrella term (the impact-page and additionality-page patterns plus scarcity, new build / newness / vintage / asset age, "drive new / additional", "real emissions reductions", and SSS near incrementality words), plus everyone who answered Q152, the survey's "balance integrity, impact and feasibility" question, read in full.
  • Coding: each respondent's passages (±400 characters, capped at 9,000) were coded once against reference/mbm_impact_codebook.md on four separate questions: stance on system impact in the MBM; whether more than SSS is needed; whether the proposal goes far enough on impact; and position on consequential accounting. Hourly matching and deliverability are counted as an impact mechanism only when the respondent links them to driving reductions, not to accuracy alone.
  • Hourly and deliverability: Q71 and Q83, 1–5 support scales; 4–5 counts as support. SSS: Q97.
  • Explicit vs implied: support coded with low confidence is reported as implied and kept out of the explicit counts.
  • Checks: AI coders; hand review of every "replace the MBM" and "oppose" code and a random 20 of the "different approach" supporters; shared templates given one code (reference/mbm_impact_overrides.csv); results compared with the additionality and impact pages (—). No second-coder agreement score.
  • Names: as entered in the consultation, cleaned where the field held a job title. Redacted and individual filers are counted, never named.
  • Files: data/derived/mbm_impact_stance.csv, reference/mbm_impact_codebook.md, scripts/analytics/mbm_impact.py.
