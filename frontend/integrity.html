What the classification does and does not tell you. A reclassification is a statement about what an organisation is, drawn from its legal form and its own description of itself — not a statement about whether it answered honestly or whether its argument is sound. Several of the largest moves are unremarkable on inspection: a certificate-standards body that is a UK limited company, a trade association that files under Other, an operating company that picked Industry group because it belongs to one. The dropdown had 15 options and no definitions attached; picking the wrong one is an easy mistake to make in both directions.

Two figures from PLAN §3.6 belong here but are not derivable from the published JSON, and are cited rather than computed: of the 44 filings that selected Academia/research, 31 answered as individuals rather than on behalf of an institution, and 10 are redacted. The responding-as × org-type cross-tabulation is not in respondents.json, so this page cannot recompute it.

Coordinated blocs

Groups of filings that answered the seven MBM anchors identically and share verbatim free text. A bloc is a record of alignment — organisations that examined the same proposals and arrived at the same position, then said so in the same words. That is what an organised stakeholder group is for. Coordination happens on every side of this consultation, and the cards below apply the same five measurements to each bloc regardless of which way it points.

Loading blocs…

How to read a bloc card. The signature strip is the group's shared answer to each of the seven anchors; a hatched bar means members did not all give the same answer there, so no shared value is claimed. Shared verbatim texts counts the clustered strings that at least two members filed identically. Identical-vector run is the longest run of scale answers on which members were byte-identical, and is reported only when a vector cluster's named members are a subset of the bloc's — a matching signature alone is not treated as evidence of the same group, which is why two blocs show none.

Named members are listed because they chose to be named. Redacted members appear only as +N redacted: they are counted so the bloc's size is right, and they are not named, guessed at, or characterised. One bloc here has no named members at all, and nothing further is said about it.

Membership of a bloc says nothing about the quality of its argument. A point drafted jointly by twelve organisations and filed twelve times is exactly as sound, or as weak, as the same point filed once — coordination is a fact about the process, not a defect in the reasoning. Several blocs here make arguments that hold up independently of how many parties signed them: registry readiness, regional equity in the fossil fallback, and misallocation under annual matching are all assessed on the Evidence & Research page on their merits, without reference to who filed them or how many times.

Template prevalence

A shared string is a normalised answer of 200+ characters filed verbatim by three or more respondents. Coordinated drafting is a legitimate and common way to answer a consultation, and shared text tells you that organisations aligned on a position — not that the position is weak.

Reused text is the weakest signal on this page. Read it last. Whether a submission deserves weight turns on things this panel does not measure: the quality of the reasoning, the evidence actually cited, the respondent's standing and track record in the subject, and the commercial exposure behind the position. Those are the informative signals, and they live elsewhere on the site — Evidence & Research covers citation rates, the declared evidence basis for each claim and which citations are peer-reviewed versus advocacy-hosted; the commercial fingerprint below covers interest; the classification audit above covers what each organisation actually is. Duplicate text ranks below all of them. It is reported here for one narrow and purely arithmetic reason: a count of filings is not a count of organisations, so any “N respondents said X” headline needs to say which of the two it means. That is a caveat about counting, not about the argument being counted.

What this audit does not claim

The limits of everything above, stated as plainly as the findings.

Read these seven points as part of the findings, not as a disclaimer appended to them.
  • Redaction is permitted, and unverifiable is not a finding. The consultation allowed respondents to withhold their identity, and 573 of 1,072 did. Those filings are recorded as unverifiable because there is nothing to check — not because anything is suspected. No redacted respondent is named, guessed at, inferred from a bloc's other members, or described here beyond a count. A redacted filing is as valid a submission as a named one.
  • Coordination is lawful consultation behaviour, on every side. Circulating a drafting pack, agreeing a common position, or filing as an association with its members is how organised stakeholders participate. It is not misconduct and nothing on this page says it is. The blocs are named because their size changes what a response count measures — mobilisation rather than independent agreement — and that applies identically to the bloc at the supportive end and the families at the opposing end.
  • Duplication does not weaken an argument, and this page does not rank submissions by it. Shared text is evidence of alignment between organisations, which is real information, and a point filed twelve times is neither stronger nor weaker than the same point filed once. The signals that actually bear on how much weight a submission deserves are the quality of its reasoning, the evidence it cites, the respondent's standing in the subject and the interest behind the position — all of which sit above reused wording, and none of which this panel measures. Treat the template counts as a note on arithmetic and go to Evidence & Research for the substance.
  • Classification is not accusation. An audited class is a statement about what an organisation is, based on its legal form and its own published description. It says nothing about honesty, and nothing about whether the organisation's argument is right. Most reclassifications here look like a respondent picking the nearest of 15 undefined dropdown options. Terms like front group and astroturf assert a motive this data cannot establish, and they appear nowhere on this page.
  • Interest is not disqualification. The fingerprint panel cuts in both directions by construction: a vendor supporting a requirement it would be paid to meet and a reporter opposing a requirement it would pay to meet are the same kind of fact. Whether hourly matching improves Scope 2 accounting is an empirical question that no count of who benefits can answer.
  • Small bases move. Several org types here rest on fewer than 25 answers per anchor, and some audited classes on fewer than 10. Every mean on this page is shown with its own n for that reason. Where a base is small, treat the direction as suggestive and the exact value as noise.
  • The dedup is partial and the page says where it stops. Duplicate filings can only be collapsed for named respondents, because a redacted respondent's individual answers are not published. The deduplicated Q71 figures therefore cover named filings only, and no estimate is offered for what dedup would do to the published 70.2%. The true number of distinct organisations behind 1,072 filings is unknowable from this data — it is lower than 1,072, and by more than the 23 duplicate filings this page can identify.

Every figure on this page, and where it comes from

  • data/audit.json — the claimed × audited matrix, the lead stat cards, the drill table and its per-row basis. Named filings only.
  • data/clusters.json — bloc membership, signatures, shared-text counts, identical-vector runs, the shared-string table and the entity families.
  • data/respondents.json — the template flag, the 7-anchor vectors and the bloc/family tags used for the Q71 dedup.
  • data/likert.json — every mean and base in the fingerprint panel and the published Q71 distribution.
  • data/meta.json — segment labels, the audited-class vocabulary and the population totals used as denominators.
  • Two exceptions, flagged where they appear: the 155-respondent template figure and the 31-of-44 academia figure come from data/derived/respondent_flags.csv and PLAN §3.6, because neither can be recomputed from the published JSON.

Nothing on this page is hardcoded except those two cited figures and the neutral one-line description on each bloc card; every count, share, mean and delta is computed from the files above when the page loads.
