What the record shows

The consultation has no question on additionality or incrementality. Every mention below was volunteered in free text.

Mentioned by a quarter, supported by a fifth, opposed by almost no one

269 respondents (25.1%) used additionality or incrementality language somewhere in their free text. Read in context, 227 of them (21.2%) treat it as something Scope 2 accounting should require, reward or be judged against. Only 13 (1.2%) argue against it, and they make a single argument: additionality is a consequential concept that does not belong in an attributional inventory. Another 15 value it but want it kept out of the inventory, and 14 mention it without taking a position.

Support for the concept is close to uncontested. The fight is over what it implies.

Same goal, opposite camps

"Additionality" is mostly invoked against the proposed revisions. Of the 199 respondents who support additionality, 107 opposed both hourly matching and deliverability, and only 41 supported both. Their argument: long-term PPAs under annual matching build more new capacity than 24/7 matching, which would divert money into administration and short-term certificates.

"Incrementality" belongs to the other camp. Of its 69 supporters, 48 backed both hourly matching and deliverability. Several of them call this the "three pillars": hourly matching, deliverable market boundaries, and an incrementality test (Standard Supply Service or similar) together.

So a headline that says "21% want additionality" hides two incompatible positions. Read alone, it can be cited by either side.

The full "three-pillar" position is a small, coordinated minority

61 respondents (5.7%) score both hourly matching (Q71) and deliverability (Q83) at 4–5 and support additionality or incrementality in their own words. Most of the 163 who back both proposals (102, or 63%) do not voice support for either concept in their free text.

This group is NGO-led (19 of 61), against a company-led opposite camp (65 of the 114 concept supporters who oppose both proposals). And 35 of the 61 share a mention sentence verbatim with at least two other respondents. The two biggest are "Incrementality, or the incentive to drive new clean electricity onto the grid…" (10 respondents) and "an incrementality standard (SSS or similar) are critical…" (9). Coordinated filing is legitimate, but it means the number of independent voices is nearer 30–35 than 61.

Read these counts as floors. They only count respondents who used the words. A respondent who asked for asset-age limits or "new-build only" without writing "additional" or "incremental" is not counted, so every support figure here is a lower bound.

How this was measured

  • Finding mentions. A keyword pass over all 25,118 free-text answers. Additionality: "additionality", "non-additional", or "additional" followed by new clean supply ("additional renewable capacity"). Incrementality: "incrementality", or "incremental" followed by clean supply or emission reductions. Bare "additional" ("additional comments") and "incremental cost" never count.
  • Coding stance. Each matched respondent's passages (±500 characters around every match) were read against a fixed codebook (reference/additionality_codebook.md) by AI coders and labelled support, oppose, mixed or neutral for each concept. Opposing hourly matching is not opposing additionality; the codebook says so explicitly.
  • Checks. All 13 oppose codes and a random 13 support or mixed codes were checked by hand against the source text. No second-coder agreement score was computed. Coders rated their own confidence: —.
  • Hourly matching and deliverability. Q71 and Q83, 1–5 support scales. Support = 4 or 5.
  • Shared wording. A respondent counts as sharing wording if the sentence containing their mention appears verbatim in at least three respondents' answers.
  • Reproduce. scripts/analytics/additionality.py regenerates the mention set and this page's data from data/derived/additionality_stance.csv.
