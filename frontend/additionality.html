In brief

  • Raised unprompted by a quarter. The survey has no additionality question. 269 respondents (25.1%) raised it anyway: 227 (21.2%) supported additionality or incrementality and 13 (1.2%) opposed it.
  • Supporters split on the proposals. Of the 227, 114 oppose both hourly matching and deliverability, 61 support both, 24 are mixed and 28 skipped one of the questions. Most "additionality" supporters are in the opposing camp; most "incrementality" supporters back both.
  • Narrowing to all three: 61 (5.7%). They support hourly matching, deliverability and additionality or incrementality. NGOs are the largest group (19), and 35 of the 61 share wording with at least two other filers.
  • SSS in total: 364 supported or were neutral (69.7% of the 522 who answered Q97; 34.0% of all respondents). 254 supported it, 110 were neutral and 158 opposed it. Every one of the 47 all-three respondents who answered Q97 supports SSS.
  • As the incrementality test, SSS splits the all-three group. In their own words, 27 respondents name SSS as the test (25 of them in the all-three group), while 26 say it needs to go further, usually with an asset-age limit (22 in the all-three group).
  • A required asset-age test comes from the other side. 15 respondents ask for one; 12 of them oppose hourly matching and deliverability and offer it as a simpler alternative.

How this was measured
  • Mentions: keyword search of all free-text answers for "additionality", "incrementality", and "additional/incremental" followed by new clean supply. "Additional comments" and "incremental cost" do not count.
  • Stance: each matching respondent's passages were read against a fixed codebook and coded support, oppose, mixed or neutral. Opposing hourly matching is not treated as opposing additionality.
  • Test named: supporters were coded a second time on SSS (backs it as the test / wants more than SSS) and on an asset age or vintage test (required / suggested). Shared template passages are coded the same way for every filer.
  • Hourly matching and deliverability: Q71 and Q83, 1–5 support scales; 4–5 counts as support.
  • Checks: coding by AI coders; every oppose and required-age-test code and a random sample of support codes were checked by hand. No second-coder agreement score.
  • Names: as entered in the consultation, cleaned where the field held a job title or description (reference/additionality_org_names.csv). Redacted and individual filers are counted, never named.
  • Files: data/derived/additionality_stance.csv, data/derived/additionality_mechanism.csv, reference/additionality_codebook.md, scripts/analytics/additionality.py.
