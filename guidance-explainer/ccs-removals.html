Is captured CO2 from a power plant a removal?

No. The Land Sector and Removals (LSR) rules treat fossil point-source capture as a prevented emission, not a removal. This page shows where that line sits, what a capturing generator, its power buyer and the storage operator each report, and where LSR stops short of scope 2 market instruments. 1 · Definitions

Three pathways, one question

LSR sorts geologically stored CO2 by where its carbon came from. Only carbon taken out of the atmosphere can be reported as a removal.

Chapter 14 of the LSR Guidance opens with two terms that sound alike and are accounted for differently. The same text appears in the LSR Standard. LSR Guidance §14.1, p.343 LSR Standard v1.1, p.77

“Captured CO2: CO2 collected at a source (preventing an emission) and that continues to be stored in a non-atmospheric carbon pool. In this case, companies do not need to account for those emissions in the relevant scope if they meet the geologic storage requirements, but this is not accounted for and reported as a removal.”

“Removal: CO2 removed directly from the atmosphere with technologies (e.g., direct air capture), or via biogenic CO2 capture (e.g., bioenergy with carbon capture and storage). These can be accounted for and reported as removals with geologic storage.”

LSR Guidance v1.0, §14.1, p.343 · source
Removal
“The transfer of a GHG from the atmosphere to storage within a non-atmospheric pool. Note: Removals can be from biological or technological sinks.”
LSR Standard v1.1 glossary · p.120
Technological sink
“A mechanical or chemical process that removes CO2 from the atmosphere or captures biogenic CO2 from a source, and stores such CO2 or other forms of carbon derived from CO2 removals in non-atmospheric carbon pools.”
LSR Standard v1.1 glossary · p.122
CO2 capture
“Collection of CO2 from a source for storage within a non-atmospheric pool.”
LSR Standard v1.1 glossary · p.113

Read the sink definition closely. It names capture of biogenic CO2 from a source. It does not mention fossil CO2. LSR Standard v1.1, p.122 Table 14.1 then answers the question directly. Its column “Does this constitute a removal?” marks “Point source fossil carbon capture and storage (CCS)” as “No”. DACCS, enhanced weathering and BECCS are marked “Yes”. LSR Guidance Table 14.1, p.350

“Captured CO2 pathways with geologic storage where the stored carbon is derived from fossil fuels or other carbon resources that are part of the long-term carbon cycle (e.g., CO2 capture of process emissions from cement production) are not accounted for as CO2 removals. Instead, capture of fossil CO2 from a point source prevents emissions if that CO2 is captured and stored.”

LSR Guidance v1.0, p.350 · source

The physical difference is concentration: ambient air at about 420 ppm (0.04 percent) against typically 3 to 20 percent in concentrated sources. The accounting difference is the carbon cycle. Stored CO2 can be a removal only “if the CO2 originates from biological or technological sinks that are part of the short-term carbon cycle”. LSR Guidance p.351 The 2004 Corporate Standard drew a similar line: sequestered atmospheric carbon “does not include GHGs captured through carbon capture and storage.” Corporate Standard 2004, glossary p.101

Both sides face the same Chapter 12 and Chapter 14 requirements (Table 14.1 note). If the origin of injected CO2 “is unknown … Requirement 21.GS is not met and removals cannot be reported.” LSR Guidance p.350, p.373

Inference · terminology

LSR says fossil CCS “prevents emissions”. It keeps “avoided emissions” for impacts outside the inventory, which “must not be included in or deducted from the company's scope 1, 2, or 3 inventory” (p.409). Describe fossil CCS as an inventory non-emission, not an avoided-emissions claim. LSR Guidance p.350, p.409

Gap · co-firing

No LSR text addresses biomass co-firing at a capture plant. Inference Equation 14.2 splits injected CO2 by fraction, so the fossil share would be a non-emission and only the biogenic share a possible removal, subject to Requirement 25. LSR Guidance p.346, p.371

2 · Reporting roles

Follow the CO2: who reports what

The Guidance assigns each stage of the storage pathway to a scope for three parties: the generator that captures, the company buying its power, and the storage operator.

Guidance §14.4.1.4 gives parallel lists for the capturing facility owner and for buyers of its electricity, steam, heat or cooling; Table 14.5 adds the storage company. These are Guidance imperatives (“Do not report…”), not Standard shall-statements, and the buyer list is not in Standard v1.1. LSR Guidance §14.4.1.4, pp.359–360; Table 14.5, p.363 LSR Standard v1.1

Every “not reported” below carries the Table 14.5 condition: “If the requirements for reporting removals or CO2 capture with geologic storage are met.” LSR Guidance Table 14.5 note a, p.363

STEP 1 · STACK

What is not captured stays in

“Any CO2 that is not captured, and all GHG emissions due to the capture process itself, must be fully accounted for.” LSR Guidance p.350

GeneratorUncaptured stack CO2 is an emission of its facility, so scope 1. Inference LSR states “must be fully accounted for” but names no scope for it.
BuyerLSR’s general scope 2 text: “Scope 2 emissions include all fossil fuel and industrial GHG emissions… released from the generation process.” p.47 Inference That covers uncaptured stack CO2.
Storage op.No role at this stage.
STEP 2 · CAPTURE AND COMPRESSION

Captured and stored: not reported

The captured tonnes drop out of the inventory; they are not subtracted. LSR Guidance pp.359–360

Generator“Do not report scope 1 emissions for the CO2 that is captured and stored in a geologic reservoir”. Fugitive captured CO2 released at the facility goes in scope 1. Show the capture “as a reduction in scope 1 emissions compared to previous years”, if the plant emitted before.
Buyer“Do not report scope 2 emissions for the CO2 that is captured and stored in a geologic reservoir”. Fugitive captured CO2 released at the facility goes in scope 2.
Storage op.No role yet.
STEP 3 · TRANSPORT

Captured CO2 is treated as a product

“companies should treat captured CO2 as a product.” At a shared hub, emissions should be allocated “based on physical allocation of the volume of CO2 supplied”. LSR Guidance p.360

GeneratorTransport of captured CO2 to the reservoir: scope 3, category 9 (Downstream transportation and distribution).
Buyer“emissions from other processes in the geologic storage pathway and any fugitive emissions of captured CO2 not occurring at the capture facility”: scope 3, category 3.
Storage op.Not addressed for this stage.
STEP 4 · INJECTION

End-of-life treatment of a sold product

The operator is “the entity that holds a well permit or license approved for CO2 storage by the relevant authorities of the jurisdiction(s).” LSR Guidance p.360

GeneratorEmissions from injection into geologic reservoirs: scope 3, category 12 (End-of-life treatment of sold products).
BuyerScope 3, category 3 (other processes in the pathway).
Storage op.Holds the permit, which commonly includes “A monitoring plan and post-closure plan” and “Proof of financial security”.
STEP 5 · RESERVOIR

Stored means still stored, and monitored

Only the portion “that remains stored” counts; losses are booked in the year they occur (section 3). LSR Guidance p.371

GeneratorFugitive captured CO2 not occurring at the capture facility: “the relevant scope 3 category”.
BuyerSame emissions: scope 3, category 3. The monitoring duty also reaches companies that did not report scope 2 emissions for captured CO2 (p.365).
Storage op.Table 14.5: “No emissions reported in scope 3 for the captured and stored CO2”. The authority should review the permit after the first five years and every 10 years thereafter. pp.361, 363

Oil and gas from the reservoir count too

Generator“GHG emissions associated with oil and gas produced through enhanced oil recovery”: scope 3, category 12. p.360
Buyer19.GS says “scope 1, scope 2, and/or scope 3”. Inference Under the buyer list these are “other processes in the geologic storage pathway”, so category 3. LSR names no buyer category for EOR.
Storage op.EOR losses reduce the stored amount in Equation 14.2.

Contrast · BECCS power

A buyer of power from a biomass plant with capture may report scope 3 removals with geologic storage in category 3, if all removal requirements are met. LSR places them in scope 3, not scope 2: “removals with geologic storage associated with purchased bioenergy are accounted for and reported in scope 3, category 3.” LSR Guidance p.47, p.307; Table 14.4, p.359

3 · Conditions

The geologic-storage gate

“Not reported” is conditional. A company earns it by meeting the Chapter 12 and Chapter 14 requirements, including the four geologic-storage requirements below (20.GS applies to scope 3 claims), and keeps it only while monitoring shows the CO2 is still underground.

A company in a fossil CCS value chain “must satisfy the same requirements to not report fossil emissions of CO2 that is captured and prevented at a source and ultimately stored in geologic reservoirs.” LSR Guidance p.349 Requirement 1 brings it into scope: companies “shall follow the Land Sector and Removals Standard and Guidance if … the company chooses to account for and report CO2 removals or CO2 capture with geologic storage in the current year or has done so in previous years.” LSR Standard v1.1, Req. 1, p.5

Inference · who is in scope

From 01 Jan 2027, a fossil generator that wants to leave captured CO2 out of scope 1 is choosing to report “CO2 capture with geologic storage”. That choice puts it in scope of LSR Chapter 14. LSR Standard v1.1, p.ii, Req. 1

19
19.GS · Cradle to grave, including EOR Count all life cycle emissions along the storage pathway, cradle to grave. With EOR, also count the extraction, processing, transport, distribution, storage and combustion of the oil and gas produced. Report them in scope 1, 2 and/or 3.
Verbatim text

“If companies account for and report removals with geologic storage or CO2 capture with geologic storage in their GHG inventory, they shall meet the following requirements: • Life cycle GHG emissions: Companies shall account for all life cycle GHG emissions that occur throughout the geologic storage pathway (i.e., cradle to grave). • This includes GHG emissions, land use, and land carbon leakage (where required) from the product life cycle(s) associated with the stored CO2 or carbon, and report them in the corresponding reporting category in scope 1, scope 2, and/or scope 3. • Enhanced oil and gas recovery: For geologic storage pathways with enhanced oil and gas recovery, companies shall account for all downstream GHG emissions associated with the extraction, processing, transportation, distribution, storage, and use (i.e., combustion) of oil, natural gas, or other hydrocarbons produced from the geologic reservoir and report such emissions in scope 1, scope 2, and/or scope 3.”

The 19.GS “Operational boundary” bullet (“a single tonne of CO2 removal with geologic storage shall not be reported by more than one entity under scope 1”) is written for removals, not captured fossil CO2. LSR Standard v1.1, pp.78–81
20
20.GS · Scope 3 physical traceability A scope 3 reporter needs physical traceability to the capturing entity and to the injection and storage operator.
Verbatim text

“If companies account for and report scope 3 removals with geologic storage or CO2 capture with geologic storage in their GHG inventory, they shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if they have physical traceability (following Requirement 8) to the entity (or entities) capturing the CO2 received at the injection site or geologic storage hub system, and the entity (or entities) operating the CO2 injection site(s) and geologic storage reservoir(s).”

Applies to scope 3 claims. There is no scope 2 version (section 5). LSR Standard v1.1, 20.GS
21
21.GS · Site-specific data, with uncertainty Use data specific to the injection site, the reservoir and the CO2 inputs, and give quantitative uncertainty estimates.
Verbatim text

“…shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if net CO2 removals with geologic storage, CO2 capture with geologic storage, and life cycle GHG emissions for the CO2 stored in the geologic reservoir(s) are accounted for using data specific to the CO2 injection site(s), geologic storage reservoir(s), and CO2 inputs into the geologic storage reservoir(s). • Uncertainty: Companies shall … only if they provide quantitative uncertainty estimates.”

LSR Standard v1.1, 21.GS
23
23.GS · Permanence: monitoring and losses Document ongoing monitoring in a monitoring plan that can detect losses. Book losses in the year they occur, as fossil fuel and industrial emissions or as reversals (figure below).
Verbatim text

“• Ongoing storage monitoring: Companies shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if ongoing storage monitoring is documented in a monitoring plan to ensure carbon remains stored in geologic reservoirs and the company can detect losses of stored carbon from relevant geologic carbon pools. • Losses of stored carbon: Companies shall account for net geologic carbon stock losses of previously reported net removals or captured CO2 with geologic storage in the year the losses occur, as either: • Fossil fuel and industrial emissions, if the carbon pools are part of the GHG inventory boundary in the reporting year; or • Reversals from geologic storage, if the carbon pools are no longer in the GHG inventory boundary in the reporting year. If companies lose the ability to monitor geologic carbon stocks associated with previously reported removals, companies shall assume previously reported removals are emitted and report reversals from geologic storage.”

LSR Standard v1.1, 23.GS

Loss of monitoring for captured fossil CO2 Gap The deemed-reversal sentence of 23.GS speaks of “previously reported removals”; the Guidance at p.368 of “previously reported CO2 removals with geologic storage”. Whether losing monitoring triggers a reversal for captured fossil CO2 is not stated. Inference The “losses of stored carbon” bullet does cover captured CO2. LSR Guidance p.368

What a loss becomes

A loss is booked in the year it happens. Its label depends on whether the reservoir is still inside the reporting company’s inventory boundary. The Standard’s reporting list also names “Reversals of CO2 capture with geologic storage: Net carbon losses from geologic reservoirs associated with previously disclosed captured CO2 (no longer in the inventory boundary).” LSR Standard v1.1, p.81

Two equations, in words

“Companies that report no CO2 emissions in scope 1, scope 2, or scope 3 for CO2 that is captured and stored in geologic reservoirs must estimate any fugitive emissions of captured CO2 following Equation 14.1 and track any losses of stored carbon using Equation 14.2.” LSR Guidance §14.5.3, p.371

Equation 14.1 · fugitive emissions of captured CO2

E_CAPCO2 = CO2_CAP − CO2_STOR

Fugitive emissions of captured CO2 equal the CO2 captured minus the CO2 capture with geologic storage. Whatever is captured but does not end up stored is an emission.

Equation 14.2 · CO2 capture with geologic storage

CO2_STOR = [CO2_INJ − CO2_L − CO2_F] × f_IC

Take the CO2 injected, subtract losses from enhanced oil recovery and fugitive CO2 from the reservoir, then multiply by the fraction of injected CO2 that came from captured fossil fuel and industrial CO2.

The result “should only be reported for the portion of captured CO2 injected into a geologic reservoir that remains stored. If this value is negative for a given year, it should be reported as losses of stored carbon”. The data checklist asks for injected CO2 in the reporting year, the fraction from captured fossil fuel and industrial CO2, EOR losses if applicable, and fugitive CO2 from the reservoir. LSR Guidance p.371; Table 14.6, p.370

Should-level recommendations: monitoring plan, custody transfer, permanence

Recommendations use “should”. The monitoring plan “should include a site characterization, description of how the operations at the geologic reservoir and monitoring comply with applicable regulations, methods used to detect any fugitive CO2 emissions, frequency of monitoring, and data quality control procedures”. At each custody transfer, “companies should report the net amount of CO2 (in tonnes) at each custody transfer, where the net amount is the difference between CO2 inputs and outputs, corrected by any changes in composition of the CO2 stream, if applicable.” LSR Standard v1.1, p.82

On permanence, the Guidance notes that well-run storage “can result in zero CO2 losses from geologic storage (e.g., defined as less than 1 percent of the total injected mass lost over 100 to 1000 years).” Where liabilities pass to a state or other jurisdiction and the operator demonstrates that storage is permanent and without risks of losses, “the date and condition of the transfer of liabilities should be clarified, and post-closure monitoring may follow regulatory guidance.” LSR Guidance p.366, p.367

Gap · capture rate

LSR gives no default or typical capture rate. The scope 1 effect follows from metered captured and stored quantities, not from a percentage. LSR Guidance §14.5.3

4 · CCU and EOR

Utilization is not storage

Only geologic storage earns the non-emission treatment in Chapter 14. CO2 put into products is a temporary non-emission, and the optional product-storage category excludes fossil carbon.

“Captured CO2 emissions with product storage or use as an input for other production purposes (e.g., greenhouse fertilization) do not represent a captured CO2 pathway with geologic storage (i.e., does not include permanent storage in geologic reservoirs). Carbon captured and stored in products represents temporary storage, followed by re-release to the atmosphere at the end of the product lifecycle. Reporting product carbon storage is optional in this Standard. Companies that choose to report product carbon storage must satisfy the requirements in Chapter 15.”

LSR Guidance v1.0, p.351 · source

In Chapter 15, product storage is “reflected in a GHG inventory as a non-emission for that period of time”, and CO2 of unknown origin should “conservatively” be treated “as fossil CO2.” LSR Guidance Ch.15, p.384

The optional product carbon storage category is narrower. Requirement 24: “The net product carbon stock change shall only include biogenic and/or TCDR-based carbon in agricultural or TCDR-based products sold by the reporting company…”. LSR Guidance p.379

EOR is allowed, with the cradle-to-grave rule of 19.GS. Box 14.1: “Where the net geologic carbon stock change is positive and greater than the life cycle GHG emissions of the geologic storage with EOR pathway … the EOR can provide climate benefits. Where the net geologic carbon stock change is negative, it leads to net CO2 emissions from geologic storage.” For the displacement of non-EOR oil and gas production, the Guidance points to the Chapter 16 consequential methods. LSR Guidance Box 14.1 p.353; p.357

Inference · fossil CCU products

TCDR-based products are products whose carbon is technologically removed CO2; if origin cannot be shown, emissions go to “fossil fuel and industrial emissions”. A product made with captured power-plant CO2 is therefore not a TCDR-based product and cannot enter the optional product carbon storage category. LSR Guidance §11.4.3, p.290 LSR Standard Req. 18, p.59

5 · Limits

What LSR does not say about scope 2

LSR tells a power buyer what to leave out. It says nothing about the instruments a buyer would use to claim power from a specific capture plant.

Where LSR discusses purchased electricity in general, it writes in grid-average terms: “determining the grid-average emission factors for fossil versus biogenic CO₂ emissions associated with purchased electricity”. LSR Guidance p.48 The 2015 Scope 2 Guidance, for its part, contains no carbon-capture text at all. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 (full-text search)

“No emissions reporting for CO2 capture with geologic storage: Ongoing storage monitoring of geologic reservoirs in the value chain of the reporting company, where the company previously did not report scope 1, scope 2, or scope 3 emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage.”

LSR Guidance v1.0, p.365 · source
Book and claim
“A chain of custody model in which the transfer of specified characteristics is not connected to the physical flow of material or products through the supply chain.”
LSR Standard Table 5.2, which marks it “No” for “Demonstrates physical traceability?” · p.25

Requirement 8 adds: “Companies cannot demonstrate physical traceability using book-and-claim approaches”. LSR Standard v1.1, Req. 8, p.24

Gap · four open questions for scope 2

(a) Whether “do not report scope 2 emissions for the CO2 that is captured” applies under the location-based method, the market-based method, or both.

(b) How a buyer on a shared grid “purchases electricity from” a CCS facility: direct line, PPA, EAC or supplier-specific factor.

(c) Whether unbundled, book-and-claim certificates can carry the treatment. 20.GS requires physical traceability for scope 3 only, and Requirement 8 rules out book-and-claim for traceability.

(d) How a buyer meets 21.GS site-specific data and the p.365 monitoring duty, or reports a reversal of CO2 capture years later. LSR Guidance v1.0 LSR Standard v1.1

Inference · the implied scope 2 factor

Read together, LSR implies that a scope 2 emission factor for CCS power should exclude only captured CO2 that is stored and meets the geologic-storage requirements. It should include uncaptured stack CO2 and capture-facility fugitive emissions. Transport, injection, storage and off-site fugitive emissions go to the buyer’s scope 3 category 3, not scope 2. LSR Guidance pp.47, 359–360

Signal of direction · 30 Jun 2026

The only bridge from LSR to scope 2 found in the research is an endnote: “The GHG Protocol plans to update and align the Corporate Standard, Scope 2 Guidance, Scope 3 Standard, and Land Sector and Removals Standard where any differences exist.” LSR Standard v1.1, Req. 3 endnote 2, p.17 Separately, LSR’s interim traceability requirement “should not be interpreted as a precedent for the AMI process” and “will be revisited in future versions of this Standard to reflect the outcomes of the AMI workstream”. LSR Guidance Box 5.2, p.77

The market-instrument question, whether a CCS plant’s output can back an energy attribute certificate under the Scope 2 Guidance, is taken up on the next page.

6 · Position

What to take into a position

What is settled, what is reasoned, and what is open, for a generator or a buyer of CCS power.

  • SourcedFossil point-source CCS is not a removal. Table 14.1 answers “No”. Captured and stored CO2 is “not accounted for and reported as a removal”; it is simply not reported as an emission. LSR Guidance §14.1, Table 14.1
  • SourcedThe generator leaves captured and stored CO2 out of scope 1; the buyer leaves it out of scope 2. Capture-facility fugitive emissions stay in scope 1 and scope 2. Downstream stages go to the generator’s scope 3 categories 9 and 12 and the buyer’s scope 3 category 3. This is Guidance text, conditioned on the geologic-storage requirements. LSR Guidance pp.359–363
  • SourcedThe gate is demanding: cradle-to-grave accounting including EOR products, site-specific data with quantitative uncertainty, a documented monitoring plan, and losses booked in the year they occur as fossil emissions or reversals. LSR Standard 19.GS–23.GS
  • InferenceFrom 01 Jan 2027, a generator that excludes captured CO2 from scope 1 is in scope of LSR Chapter 14. A scope 2 factor consistent with LSR would carry uncaptured and capture-site fugitive CO2 only.
  • GapLSR says nothing on market-based versus location-based methods, EACs, PPAs, residual mix or the Quality Criteria, and nothing on how an unbundled-certificate buyer would meet the monitoring duty. The GHG Protocol “plans to update and align” the standards. LSR Standard v1.1, endnote 2, p.17
Takeaway

Under LSR, fossil CCS changes what is emitted, not what is removed. The stored tonnes drop out of scope 1 and scope 2 only while the geologic-storage requirements are met and monitoring continues, and LSR leaves open how that treatment would travel through a market instrument.
