Module 3 · Storage

How does stored electricity get counted hour by hour?

A store is a load when it charges and a source when it discharges, and it loses energy in between. After this page you can say what GHG Protocol guidance does and does not say about that, and how third-party hourly ledgers handle it.

1 · The net-load problem

A store is load, then source, with losses in between

The in-force guidance has no storage rule. The nearest anchors are its rules on network losses, on on-site generation, and on double counting.

Start with what is missing. A full-text search of the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance for storage terms finds one passing mention of storage technologies and nothing on how storage charging, discharging or round-trip losses are treated. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Box 4.1, p.27

“Improved short-term forecasting of variable resources and storage technologies will also minimize these challenges.”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), Box 4.1, p.27 · source
Gap

The in-force guidance says nothing about how stored electricity, charging consumption, discharge, or storage losses are treated in either the location-based or the market-based method. “Net load” is not defined in any GHG Protocol source the research reviewed.

The figure walks through one illustrative day for a grid-connected store. Each step names the 2015 text that comes closest, and marks where the reasoning goes beyond it.

STEP 1

Charging is a withdrawal

Scope 2 covers emissions from the generation of electricity “purchased or acquired … consumed by the reporting company”. Scope 2 consultation draft 2025, p.7 (quoting 2015 §5.3)

Inference

When the store charges it draws from the grid. In these terms that is consumption.

STEP 2

Discharge is an injection, not generation

Quality Criterion 1 speaks of “the direct GHG emission rate attribute associated with the unit of electricity produced.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Table 7.1, p.60

Inference

A discharged MWh has no direct emission rate of its own. The only coherent attribute for it is the one inherited from the energy that was charged. Call that “pass-through”.

STEP 3

Losses are energy truly consumed

For networks, the guidance counts losses as consumed energy and gives an example: “7 percent grid loss rate for 100MWh consumption would mean 7MWh lost in T&D”. These losses sit in scope 3, category 3, and may be covered by certificates under the market-based method. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, §4.2 p.27; App. B, pp.96–97

Inference

By analogy, round-trip losses are energy consumed by whoever operates the store. No source says so, and none says whose scope 2 carries them.

STEP 4

Where the 2015 gross rule bites

For on-site generation the guidance is explicit: “companies shall use the total—or gross—electricity purchases from the grid rather than grid purchases "net" of generation”. Its worked example gives 120 kWh total consumption against 20 kWh “net” grid consumption. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, §5.4 and Table 5.1, pp.38–39

Inference

Read across to a grid-connected store, scope 2 activity data would be gross charging energy, not charging minus discharging. Whether a discharge counts as “generation sold back to the grid” is not addressed.

A stakeholder reading, not guidance

One respondent, Tierra Climate, writes in a Q46 answer about the location-based method that “Only losses are included in Scope 2 activity data for storage”. The research found no GHG Protocol text that states this. Consultation raw feedback 2026, Tierra Climate, Q46

Double-counting anchors

“(In specific cases of multipliers or issuance of multiple instruments from the same MWh, then all instruments shall be retired for a full claim on that MWh.)”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), §7.5 Criteria 1, p.63 · source

“The underlying electricity (or megawatt-hour) minus the instrument, sometimes called "null power," shall also not reflect the same GHG emission rate, but should be assigned residual mix emissions for the purpose of delivery and/or use claims in the market-based method.”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), §7.5 Criteria 2, p.64 · source
Inference

These are the in-force anchors for a rule that a charging certificate must be cancelled if a discharge certificate is to carry its attribute. Otherwise two instruments carry a claim on the same MWh, which conflicts with Quality Criterion 2.

Age does not matter in 2015

“All energy has a direct emissions factor associated with generation, and the use of that emissions factor does not depend on whether the generation facility is existing or new, or why the generation has occurred.”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), §11.3, p.90 · source

The 2015 guidance treats the United States as a single certificate market and the EU as a multi-country market. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, §7.5, p.65

Inference

With annual certificates and markets that wide, no time-shifting device is needed. An annual certificate already covers any hour. Storage only becomes material to the market-based method once Quality Criterion 4 is hourly.

2 · The yardstick

What an hourly storage ledger would have to satisfy

Any storage rule would be tested against the in-force Quality Criteria, the accounting principles, and the criteria proposed in the 2025 draft.

The eight Scope 2 Quality Criteria (verbatim, in force)

“All contractual instruments used in the market-based method for scope 2 accounting shall:”

  1. “Convey the direct GHG emission rate attribute associated with the unit of electricity produced.”
  2. “Be the only instruments that carry the GHG emission rate attribute claim associated with that quantity of electricity generation.”
  3. “Be tracked and redeemed, retired, or canceled by or on behalf of the reporting entity.”
  4. “Be issued and redeemed as close as possible to the period of energy consumption to which the instrument is applied.”
  5. “Be sourced from the same market in which the reporting entity's electricity-consuming operations are located and to which the instrument is applied.”
    In addition, utility-specific emission factors shall:
  6. “Be calculated based on delivered electricity, incorporating certificates sourced and retired on behalf of its customers. Electricity from renewable facilities for which the attributes have been sold off (via contracts or certificates) shall be characterized as having the GHG attributes of the residual mix in the utility or supplier-specific emission factor.”
    In addition, companies purchasing electricity directly from generators or consuming on-site generation shall:
  7. “Ensure all contractual instruments conveying emissions claims be transferred to the reporting entity only. No other instruments that convey this claim to another end user shall be issued for the contracted electricity. The electricity from the facility shall not carry the GHG emission rate claim for use by a utility, for example, for the purpose of delivery and use claims.”
    Finally, to use any contractual instrument in the market-based method requires that:
  8. “An adjusted, residual mix characterizing the GHG intensity of unclaimed or publicly shared electricity shall be made available for consumer scope 2 calculations, or its absence shall be disclosed by the reporting entity.”
Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Table 7.1, p.60
The five accounting principles (verbatim, in force)

“GHG accounting and reporting shall be based on the following principles:”

  • “Relevance. Ensure the GHG inventory appropriately reflects the GHG emissions of the company and serves the decision-making needs of users—both internal and external to the company.”
  • “Completeness. Account for and report on all GHG emission sources and activities within the inventory boundary. Disclose and justify any specific exclusion.”
  • “Consistency. Use consistent methodologies to allow for meaningful performance tracking of emissions over time. Transparently document any changes to the data, inventory boundary, methods, or any other relevant factors in the time series.”
  • “Transparency. Address all relevant issues in a factual and coherent manner, based on a clear audit trail. Disclose any relevant assumptions and make appropriate references to the accounting and calculation methodologies and data sources used.”
  • “Accuracy. Ensure that the quantification of GHG emissions is systematically neither over nor under actual emissions, as far as can be judged, and that uncertainties are reduced as far as practicable. Achieve sufficient accuracy to enable users to make decisions with reasonable confidence as to the integrity of the reported information.”

“Companies may encounter tradeoffs between principles when completing an inventory and should strike a balance between these principles based on their individual business goals.”

Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Ch. 3, p.21

Below, each requirement pairs its anchor text (Source) with what it would imply for storage (Inference). The eight items follow this site's derivation from the criteria above. None of them is GHG Protocol text about storage.

01

Cancel on charge

Source QC2: “Be the only instruments that carry the GHG emission rate attribute claim associated with that quantity of electricity generation.” QC3: tracked and retired. Multiple instruments from one MWh: “all instruments shall be retired for a full claim on that MWh.” 2015 Table 7.1, p.60; §7.5, p.63

Inference

Charging certificates would be cancelled “for charging” in the charge hour. That guards unique claims and is auditable, but it needs registry capability.

02

Haircut for losses

Source Accuracy: “systematically neither over nor under actual emissions”. Completeness: “Account for and report on all GHG emission sources and activities”. 2015 Ch. 3, p.21

Inference

Discharge certificates would equal measured output, not input. The loss quantity is consumption. It needs certificates, or a residual-mix or fossil factor under the proposed residual-mix criterion. Whether losses fall on the operator or the end claimant is open. Gap

03

Two hours, two matches

Source Proposed QC4: instruments “issued and redeemed for the same hour as the energy consumption to which the instrument is applied”. 2025 draft §5.1.1, p.20

Inference

Pass-through creates two consumptions: the store's charging in hour tc and the end user's in hour td. The charging certificate matches tc; the discharge certificate matches td. No certificate crosses hours unbacked.

04

Deliverability on both legs

Source Proposed QC5: generation “deemed deliverable to the consuming load”; a facility “shall be considered as located at its first point of interconnection to a transmission network.” 2025 draft §5.1.2, p.23

Inference

Two legs: generator to store, then store to load. A store behind the generator's connection meets leg 1 trivially. A standalone store must sit in the same deliverable boundary. The draft does not say which location a discharge instrument carries. Gap

05

No cherry-picking

Source Completeness: “Disclose and justify any specific exclusion.” Transparency: “a clear audit trail”. 2015 Ch. 3, p.21

Inference

All discharge from a registered store would be recorded. Uncovered discharge would go to an “unknown beneficiary” (the EnergyTag approach) or to the residual mix.

06

Published allocation order

Source Transparency and Consistency principles. 2015 Ch. 3, p.21

Inference

Whether charge records are allocated first-in-first-out, by weighted average, or at the operator's choice is a transparency and consistency question. GHG Protocol is silent on it. Gap

07

Grid-charged storage

Source Proposed residual mix: “not claimed through contractual instruments or allocated via Standard Supply Service”; it “should reflect the highest temporal precision available”. 2025 draft, Criterion 9, p.27

Inference

Charging not covered by cancelled clean certificates carries no clean attribute. Either the stored energy inherits the charge-hour residual mix, or no attribute is issued on discharge and the receiving consumer falls to its own residual mix. No residual-mix rule that tracks storage is proposed. Gap

08

Netting within an hour

Source The draft accepts that sub-hourly dispatch is a trade-off: “hourly matching of certificates as a practical cadence for accurate market-based allocation.” 2025 draft, rationale p.29

Inference

One net state per metering interval of 60 minutes or less fits the hourly cadence. The 2015 gross-purchase “shall” suggests charge and discharge are not netted against each other across hours in activity data.

3 · Ledger mechanics

How third-party schemes run the ledger

The working mechanics exist only outside the GHG Protocol: in the EnergyTag certificate standard and in two hydrogen regulations.

EnergyTag explains why older certificate systems did not need storage rules, and why hourly ones do. The Standard makes storage certificates optional for issuers: an issuer “may issue Storage Discharge GCs”, and if it does it must follow the storage section. EnergyTag GC Scheme Standard v2, pp.10, 25

“For most historic EACs systems, cancellation for energy Input to storage is not required. The logic was that storage is not end-consumption and, in most cases the "clean-up" of storage losses was voluntarily dealt with by canceling the corresponding number of EACs if specific Attributes were claimed for the stored energy. GCs related to energy fed into and released from storage cannot be managed like this, as the time interval in which the energy is charged and discharged is of essential value. EnergyTag acknowledges that storage tracking is complex and is still in an early stage of development and implementation.”

EnergyTag GC Scheme Standard v2, §1.6, p.25 · source

1 · Production. A solar device produces 100 Wh between 17:00 and 18:00 on 15 Jan 2024 and a GC is issued. The store is treated as “both a Production Device and a Consumption Point”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.24, 28

2 · Cancel on charge. “GCs for energy produced in the same time interval as the SCR shall be canceled, indicating that this cancellation is "for charging" energy into a Storage System.” A Storage Charge Record (SCR) records the metered input. Intervals “shall be 60 minutes or less”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.27, 29

3 · Losses come off. “SCRs represent a quantity of charged energy that shall be allocated to SDRs except for the share related to energy losses.” The schematic shows “SCR AL (AL = After Losses) … Vol = 95 Wh”. “Parasitic losses shall be included in this overall loss factor”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.28–30, Annex 2 pp.49–50

4 · Discharge record. At 20:00–21:00 the store discharges 95 Wh, recorded as a Storage Discharge Record (SDR). SDR volume “shall be based on measurement of energy output from storage.” Each unit of an SCR “may only be allocated once to an SDR.” EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.27–30

5 · Re-issue on discharge. A Storage Discharge GC (SD-GC) for 95 Wh is issued, “always AFTER cancellation of Input GCs”. Attributes “shall be copied to SDRs” and “SD-GCs shall be Issued in equal volume as the corresponding SDRs.” The SD-GC carries a Storage Tag and records “both the location of the original Production Device … and the location of the storage device”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.28, 30

6 · Claim. The SD-GC is matched to consumption in hour td. A temporal claim needs the GC (tc), SCR (tc), SDR (td) and SD-GC (td) all “no longer available for further use”. Geographic matching “shall be done” between production device and store, and between store and end-consumption. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.29–30

Gap in the third-party scheme

Charging not covered by cancelled GCs creates records that “do not contain information regarding the energy source” (p.25). The Standard does not say whether discharge from those records carries grid-mix or residual-mix attributes. EnergyTag's FAQ says it “is currently developing a robust standard for storage accounting”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, p.25 EnergyTag FAQ

Uncertified discharge may go to an “unknown beneficiary” so the reservoir matches physical capacity, and the order of allocation must be published, with “ensuring positive environmental impact of the storage system” as the guiding principle. The Standard sets no age requirement for the store or the charged generation. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, pp.28, 52–53

Scope of the 45V precedent

The final regulations do not let hydrogen producers contract with off-site storage to shift their load profile. The storage pathway depends “on whether and when EAC registries can substantiate the effective tracking of electricity through that storage.” 45V final regulations, 90 FR 2270

Gap

The research did not verify the current statutory status of the 45V credit after 2025 legislation. It is cited here only as a regulatory design precedent. The EU text does not define “new storage asset” further.

4 · The 2025 draft and the feedback

The draft mentioned storage three ways, and set no rule

Storage appears in one definition, one sentence of rationale, and four answer options. Respondents then said a lot about it.

Gap

The October 2025 consultation draft contains no proposal on charging and discharging, storage certificates, loss allocation, or co-located storage. It also contains no incrementality, additionality, commercial-operation-date or asset-age rule. It says “additional topics are expected to be addressed in Phase 2.” Scope 2 consultation draft 2025, p.4

Consumption-based averages (proposed, location-based method)
“"Consumption-based averages" characterize the weighted-average GHG emission rate of the electricity generation consumed in a specific geographic area and time period. Consumption-based averages reflect electricity imports, exports, and stored electricity produced in a previous time period, whereas production-based averages do not.”
The only storage-specific definitional text in the draft · Scope 2 consultation draft 2025, §4.1.1, p.10

“Further, hourly price signals could create financial incentives for energy technologies (such as energy storage, clean firm power, demand response, etc.) that will be needed at scale to fully decarbonize grids.”

Market-based method rationale · Scope 2 consultation draft 2025, p.29

No question in the consultation asked about storage directly. Storage appears only as an answer option to four questions. The chart shows how many respondents to each question chose it. Consultation raw data (question_options.csv)

What respondents said

“When batteries charge, they should consume GOs according to their power consumption and energy mix. When discharging the power, batteries should then receive GOs according to the power delivered to the grid.”

Ørsted A/S · Company, Denmark · Q133 · raw feedback

“At present, reporting and regulatory requirements for battery & storage systems are not fully established in all markets where we operate, for example, how losses during charge/discharge are to be accounted for, how GO's can be issued for electricity that has passed via a storage system and what meter boundary should be used for co-located BESS+PV/wind.”

Sonnedix UK Services Ltd · Energy supplier/retailer or utility, UK · Q020 · raw feedback

“Merchant storage or independently operated storage will have positive emissions in MBM inventories unless it purchases EACs to cover losses, even when it reduces system emissions on a consequential basis. MBM, like LBM, fundamentally assigns emissions based on losses, not consequential changes to the grid.”

Tierra Climate Inc. · Data/analytics provider, US · Q073 · raw feedback

“But for other grids and portfolios, storage resources dispatched to "fill the hourly gaps" in a company's renewable portfolio will increase fossil generation when they charge. Under these circumstances, due to the efficiency losses inherent in energy storage systems, storage deployed to reduce a company's reported emissions will have the effect of increasing atmospheric emissions.”

Redacted · Company, US · Q075 · raw feedback

“Hourly matching would specifically incentivize companies not to produce clean energy or discharge storage in these hours beyond their load. This would actively increase short-run emissions; cause grid stress; and slow renewables build in the long run.”

WattTime · NGO, US · Q075 · raw feedback
Template-text caution

Fourteen respondents (8 companies, 2 consultants, 2 industry groups, 1 academia, 1 data provider) used near-identical wording: hourly matching “can incentivize behavior, such as the timing of charging and discharging of energy storage projects, that align with an individual reporting entities carbon accounting needs at the expense of system-level benefits”. This looks like shared text. Do not count it as fourteen independent views. Consultation raw data (keyword search)

GHG Protocol's own summaries record the themes, not a position. On the separate consequential consultation, “Some respondents noted that consequential accounting is better suited to recognize certain technologies like battery storage and demand response, which attributional methods can only represent as a net emissions increase.” Consequential Impacts Summary of Feedback, 29 Jul 2026, p.3

Gap

Neither feedback summary reports a GHG Protocol position on storage mechanics.

5 · Position

What to take into a position

Keep three things apart: what the texts say, what follows from them, and what nobody has decided.

Takeaway

No GHG Protocol text, in force or in revision, sets a storage ledger. On the research's reading (inference), the Quality Criteria point toward cancel on charge, re-issue on discharge net of measured losses, and two hourly matches. Only third-party schemes have written that down.

  • Sourced The 2015 guidance has no storage rule. It requires gross grid purchases for on-site generation, counts T&D losses as consumption, and says emission factors do not depend on whether a facility “is existing or new”. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, pp.38, 96, 90
  • Sourced EnergyTag cancels certificates on charge, keeps charge and discharge records per interval of 60 minutes or less, and, where an issuer opts in, re-issues on discharge net of measured losses, recording both locations. 45V counts stored energy in the discharge hour and tests the generator, not the store. EU 2023/1184 requires a “new storage asset”. EnergyTag v2 45V EU 2023/1184
  • Sourced The 2025 draft mentions storage only in a definition, its rationale, and answer options to Q45, Q72, Q140 and Q147. Scope 2 consultation draft 2025
  • Inference Quality Criterion 2 and the multiple-instrument rule imply that a charging certificate must be cancelled before a discharge certificate can carry its attribute. Accuracy implies a haircut for measured losses.
  • Gap Whose scope carries storage losses; how uncertified discharge and grid charging enter an hourly residual mix; which location a discharge instrument carries; and whether charge and discharge may be netted within one hour behind one meter.
Inference

A position on storage mechanics can be argued from in-force criteria (QC2, QC3, Accuracy, Completeness) and the proposed QC4 and QC5. It cannot cite a GHG Protocol storage rule, because none exists yet. The next page covers where the revision has assigned the topic.
