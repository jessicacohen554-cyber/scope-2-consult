Does a CCS retrofit get plant credit or system credit?

No GHG Protocol document answers this directly. This page assembles the answer from in-force building blocks, case by case: retrofit vs new build, coal vs gas, storage vs utilization. Most of it is labelled inference, because that is what it is.

Retrofit × Statement 1 Source

“Do not report scope 1 emissions for the CO2 that is captured and stored in a geologic reservoir; • Report any fugitive emissions of captured CO2 released at that facility in scope 1; • Report emissions from other processes in the geologic storage pathway and any fugitive emissions of captured CO2 not occurring at the capture facility in the relevant scope 3 category; • Reflect the captured CO2 as a reduction in scope 1 emissions compared to previous years, if CO2 was emitted from the facility in prior years.”

LSR v1.0 Ch.14, owner or controller of the capture facility, p.359 · source

“The impact of CO2 capture is reflected in a GHG inventory by not reporting emissions that have instead been prevented. … Any CO2 that is not captured, and all GHG emissions due to the capture process itself, must be fully accounted for.”

LSR Ch.14 p.350 · source

It is a prevented emission, not a removal: captured fossil CO2 “is not accounted for and reported as a removal.” LSR Ch.14 p.343 Under the Corporate Standard Ch.8 precedent, such internal reductions “need not be reported separately unless they are sold, traded externally, or otherwise used as an offset or credit.” AMI White Paper Annex C, p.48

Retrofit × Statement 3 Inference

“For these project activities, baseline emissions from the continuation of current activities may be estimated as the historical GHG emission rate for the process or equipment that is being retrofitted.”

Project Protocol Box 8.7, p.58 · source

“If a piece of equipment would have to be replaced in the near future, then in practical terms this would constitute an absolute barrier to the continuation of current activities, and the continuation of current activities should not be considered as a baseline scenario alternative.”

Project Protocol Box 8.2, p.52 · source

The Grid Guidelines class a reduction that occurs “at the coal-fired power plant itself” as not grid-connected and send it to “Project Protocol general procedures”. Grid Guidelines Table 2.1, p.12

Inference

At-plant effect ≈ (historical unabated rate − abated rate) × the plant’s generation. Lost or added output is a separate grid effect at OM/BM. If the plant was near the end of its life, the baseline becomes the replacement generation, and the whole impact becomes a system change. That end-of-life test is the hinge.

“Special treatment may therefore be needed to separately recognize: 1) the direct cessation of emissions from the unit (using observed and verifiable data), and 2) the consequential effects associated with replacement generation and capacity decisions.”

The NorthBridge Group (consultant), Q22 · Feedback dataset, respondent 96

New build × Statement 1 Source Inference

The same LSR rules apply: captured and geologically stored CO2 is not reported in scope 1; residual stack CO2, capture-process emissions and fugitives at the facility are. “Any CO2 that is not captured, and all GHG emissions due to the capture process itself, must be fully accounted for.” LSR Ch.14 pp.350, 359

Inference

The “reduction … compared to previous years” rule only bites “if CO2 was emitted from the facility in prior years”. A new plant has no prior years, so its owner’s scope 1 total rises by the residual, process and fugitive emissions.

New build × Statement 3 Inference

The Grid Guidelines treat a new fossil plant as a grid-connected generation project: “Install and operate grid-connected natural gas combined-cycle power plant | Generate low-emission electricity from high-efficiency natural gas plant | Reduce combustion GHG emissions from (higher emitting) grid-connected power plants”. The baseline rate is “ER_baseline = wBM + (1-w)OM”. Grid Guidelines Table 2.1, Eq.1, pp.12–13

“Generally, project activities that can provide firm power at all times will fully displace the BM (i.e., w = 1).”

Grid Guidelines §5.3, p.32 · source

The consultation’s default table gives “Project provides firm power, and on-peak, baseload, or intermittent generation: 1”. Consultation p.17

Inference

Impact ≈ generation × (ER_baseline − residual rate), plus or minus secondary effects. A firm CCS plant would typically get w = 1, so the build margin drives the sign.

“The proposed Scope 2 TWG subgroup formula does not address the emissions impact from actions that produce incremental emissions, such as building a new natural gas-fired power plant to serve load.”

Constellation Energy (energy supplier/utility), Q20 · Feedback dataset, respondent 181

Footnote: whether the same change may appear in Statement 1 and Statement 3 is unresolved. Each statement is reported separately, “without netting”. AMI White Paper pp.28, 35 RFI Summary p.4

Inference · the short answer

A retrofit gets both kinds of credit, split into separate effects. The rate cut is a plant-level effect against the plant’s own history. Any change in output, dispatch or lifetime is a system effect valued at the grid margin. A new build gets only a system-change credit.

For the plant owner, the draft framework points to Statement 3’s “within organizational boundary impacts”. By analogy, the Explanatory Memo’s CHP example sits there and “also has implication for Scope 1 reporting in the physical GHG inventory”. AMI White Paper p.22 Explanatory Memo p.10

2 · Retrofit

Retrofit walk-through, gas then coal

Nine steps from the baseline to the EOR branch. The bars use NETL design data for new plants, because no retrofit data was retrieved.

Gap · data

NETL Rev 4a covers greenfield plants only: “All plant configurations were evaluated based on installation at a greenfield site.” No source here gives retrofit capture penalties. The figures below are stand-ins for the shape of the effects, not retrofit performance. NETL Rev 4a (2022) p.3

STEP 1 · BASELINE

The plant’s own history, unless it was about to close

For a retrofit that lowers an existing rate, baseline emissions “may be estimated as the historical GHG emission rate for the process or equipment that is being retrofitted.” Project Protocol Box 8.7, p.58 The exception is equipment reaching “the end of its useful lifetime”; then continued operation “should not be considered as a baseline scenario alternative.” Project Protocol Box 8.2, p.52

The brown bar stands in for the historical unabated rate.

STEP 2 · AT-PLANT PRIMARY EFFECT

Gas: 755 to 85 lb/MWh-net

In NETL’s F-class design, 90% capture takes the stack rate from 755 to 85 lb CO₂/MWh-net. NETL Rev 4a Exhibit ES-4, p.9 Per MWh that is −88.7% (derived), because the capture plant uses energy.

Inference

This is the at-plant effect: (historical rate − abated rate) × generation. In Statement 1 the same physics shows as the scope 1 drop. LSR Ch.14 p.359

STEP 3 · ENERGY PENALTY

Gas loses output; someone else generates it

NETL’s NGCC design holds fuel constant, so net output falls from 727 to 645 MW (−11.3%, derived); net efficiency falls from 53.6% to 47.6%. NETL Exhibit ES-4

Inference

By symmetry with Box 8.2’s second primary effect and Table 2.1 row 2, the lost MWh are a grid effect at the operating margin (build margin if persistent), and they increase emissions. Any OM rate used here would be hypothetical; the consultation’s only figure is an illustrative “roughly ~800 lb CO₂/MWh” for a gas generator. Consultation p.17

STEP 4 · COAL

Coal: bigger tonnes, bigger penalty

NETL’s supercritical PC goes from 1,718 to 219 lb CO₂/MWh-net at 90% capture (−87.3%, derived). Its design holds 650 MW net by raising thermal input about 27% (derived); auxiliary power rises from 36 to 113 MWe and net efficiency falls from 40.2% to 31.7%. NETL Exhibit ES-3, p.8

Inference

Holding output means no lost MWh, but the extra coal is an upstream secondary effect: one of the cases where the Guidelines’ presumption that projects “reduce or cause no increase in fuel extraction” does not obviously hold.

STEP 5 · DISPATCH

Will the plant run more, or less?

If capture revenue changes how often the plant runs, the extra or missing MWh displace, or are displaced by, marginal generation: again an OM/BM effect (inference). Grubert & Sawyer model “six scenarios for lifespan extension and capacity factor changes”, and argue that the US 45Q credit “incentivizes emitters to maximize production and sequestration of carbon oxides, not abatement.” Grubert & Sawyer 2023, abstract

STEP 6 · UPSTREAM FUEL

Methane per kWh rises with capture

IPCC AR5’s typical methane values rise from 47 to 68 gCO2eq/kWh for coal PC with CCS, and from 91 to 110 for gas combined cycle with CCS. IPCC AR5 WGIII Annex III, Table A.III.2, p.1335 The Project Protocol lists coal-mine methane and gas leaks as upstream effects. Project Protocol §5.3.2

Inference

The Grid Guidelines say fuel-chain effects “can often be ignored”; for capture with an energy penalty that presumption is weak.

STEP 7 · TRANSPORT AND STORAGE

Cradle to grave

Fugitive captured CO2 at the facility stays in scope 1; other storage-pathway emissions go to scope 3. “Companies shall account for all life cycle GHG emissions that occur throughout the geologic storage pathway (i.e., cradle to grave).” Transport falls in scope 3 category 9; injection in category 12. LSR Ch.14 pp.344, 359–360

STEP 8 · LIFETIME AND LOCK-IN

If capture keeps a plant open

Grubert & Sawyer’s scenarios change emissions “by −24% ($0.4 trillion) to +82% ($3.6 trillion) versus business-as-usual for affected generators.” Grubert & Sawyer 2023, abstract A reply to a DOE commentary keeps the dispute open: “We share a belief that many of these loopholes can be closed, and disagree that they have already been closed.” Grubert & Sawyer reply, abstract

Inference

For years the plant would not otherwise have run, the baseline is the replacement mix (Box 8.2 end-of-life barrier). With a clean build margin, those years can be a net increase.

STEP 9 · EOR BRANCH

When the CO₂ goes to oil recovery

Companies “shall account for all downstream GHG emissions associated with the extraction, processing, transportation, distribution, storage, and use (i.e., combustion) of oil, natural gas, or other hydrocarbons produced from the geologic reservoir”. LSR Req. 19.GS, p.344 Chapter 16 methods “can be applied to quantify the impacts of EOR production, which would include the displacement of non-EOR oil and gas production.” LSR Ch.14 p.357

3 · New build

New build: a system-change credit whose sign can flip

A new plant has no history to beat. Its impact is the margin it displaces minus what it still emits.

Inference

A new CCS plant is analogous to the new-NGCC grid-connected generation row in Grid Guidelines Table 2.1. Baseline emissions are then the margin rate times the project’s generation; project emissions are the plant’s residual rate times the same generation, which is not zero. Grid Guidelines Table 2.1, §12.2.2

The consultation notes “For renewable energy generation the project scenario Primary Effect emissions are usually zero”. Consultation p.7

Fossil-heavy case: the only GHG Protocol number is the consultation’s illustrative “build margin impact of roughly ~2,000 lb CO2/MWh” for a coal retirement. It is not data. Consultation p.17 Residual rates: NETL Rev 4a ES-3, ES-4

Mostly clean case: the near-zero bar is illustrative, not a sourced margin; that the impact can turn negative is inference. Residual rates: NETL Rev 4a ES-3, ES-4

Inference

Because a firm CCS plant would typically take w = 1, the build margin, not the operating margin, decides the sign. An alternative project-specific baseline is “implementation of a baseline candidate”: an unabated plant of the same type. Then the effect is roughly (unabated rate − abated rate) × generation, and the additionality question moves to whether capture would have been built anyway. Grid Guidelines §5.3, p.32 Project Protocol §2.8, p.12

One-time effects, and a respondent on lifetime impact

One-time effects net out partly: “if a project activity displaces construction of another power plant – in other words, if it affects the build margin – then similar GHG emissions would also occur in the baseline scenario”. Inference: what remains is the extra construction for capture equipment, pipelines and storage wells. Grid Guidelines §4.3.1, p.27

“People often underestimate the full cumulative negative GHG emissions lifetime impact of setting up a new gas-fired powerplant that is likely to operate for the next 20-30 years.”

Vidia Equity (financial institution), Q18 · Feedback dataset, respondent 25

Inference

Coal capture removes more CO₂ per MWh (1,718 → 219 vs 755 → 85 lb/MWh-net), so its at-plant primary effect per MWh is larger. Coal also has the larger efficiency penalty and the higher residual. 5 · Storage vs use

CCU vs CCS

Only geologic storage takes CO₂ out of scope 1. Utilization leaves it in the inventory and changes what the impact baseline must be.

Geologic storage (CCS)

Statement 1

“CO2 capture with geologic storage that meets the geologic storage requirements is not reported as emissions in the relevant scope.” LSR Ch.14 p.347

Life cycle

Cradle to grave across the storage pathway (Req. 19.GS). LSR Ch.14 p.344

Permanence

Monitoring, with reversals reported as “Net carbon losses from geologic reservoirs associated with previously disclosed captured CO2”. LSR Ch.14 p.347

Statement 3 baseline Inference

Historical rate (retrofit) or margin (new build), as above.

Geologic storage with EOR

Statement 1

Storage, but produced oil and gas emissions are counted in scope 1, 2 and/or 3. LSR Req. 19.GS, p.344

Life cycle

“the full life cycle of the produced oil or natural gas must be considered to determine the net CO2 flux”. LSR Box 14.1, pp.352–353

Permanence

“Where the net geologic carbon stock change is negative, it leads to net CO2 emissions from geologic storage.” LSR Box 14.1

Statement 3 baseline

Ch.16 methods, including “the displacement of non-EOR oil and gas production”. LSR Ch.14 p.357

Utilization (CCU)

Statement 1

Not a captured-CO2 pathway: “Carbon captured and stored in products represents temporary storage, followed by re-release to the atmosphere at the end of the product lifecycle.” LSR Ch.14 p.351

Life cycle

“Fossil CO2 capture that doesn’t meet the geologic storage requirement is reported as ‘fossil fuel and industrial emissions’ in the relevant scope.” Product storage reporting is optional (Ch.15). LSR Ch.14 pp.347, 351

Permanence Inference

Enters through re-release, not storage monitoring. The AMI permanence principle “applies to removals only”. AMI White Paper p.32

Statement 3 baseline Inference

The product system the market would otherwise have used; the impact is a life-cycle difference, not the captured tonnage.

Inference

A plant that captures CO₂ for fuels, chemicals, beverages or greenhouses still reports that CO₂ in scope 1 under LSR. In Statement 3, the Project Protocol’s equivalence rule applies: “if the project activity were not implemented, it should be assumed that the market would have provided a quantity and quality of products or services equivalent to what the project activity would have produced.” The baseline is then the displaced product or CO₂ source, and the impact is the life-cycle difference including re-release. Project Protocol §2.13, pp.14–15; §5.3.2

Signal of direction · 9 Sep 2026

ISO WG4 consensus is to remove permanence as a principle, with Statement 3 referencing ISO 14064-2. AMI TWG 2.08 minutes pp.3–4

Gap

No GHG Protocol source quantifies CCU re-release timing, utilization baselines or EOR displacement rates, and no third-party number on CCU permanence was retrieved. LSR Ch.14’s pointer to Ch.16 for EOR is the only place the sources link CCS to consequential accounting.

7 · Interplay

Statement 2 vs Statement 3: who claims what

If the plant sells attributes, the buyer’s inventory claim and the plant’s impact claim are different things. The rules that would stop two impact claims on one change are not written.

8 · Closing

What to take into a position

What the text says, what follows from it, and what nobody has written yet.

Takeaway

In inventory terms (sourced) a capture retrofit cuts the owner’s scope 1. In impact terms (inference) it earns an at-plant effect against its own history only if it was not about to retire, net of lost output, fuel-chain and lifetime effects; a new build earns only a system-change credit, and a clean build margin can turn it negative.

  • SOURCED. Captured and geologically stored fossil CO₂ is not reported in scope 1 and is not a removal; capture-process, fugitive and storage-pathway emissions stay in. Utilization is “temporary storage, followed by re-release”. LSR Ch.14 pp.343, 350–351, 359
  • SOURCED. In-force project rules allow retrofits a historical-rate baseline unless the equipment is at end of life, and treat at-plant reductions as not grid-connected. Project Protocol Boxes 8.2, 8.7 Grid Guidelines Table 2.1
  • SOURCED (third party). At 90% capture NETL’s greenfield (new-build) designs cut the per-MWh rate by 87.3% (coal) and 88.7% (gas), derived; AR5 methane per kWh rises with CCS for both fuels. NETL Rev 4a IPCC AR5 Annex III
  • INFERENCE. Retrofit: plant credit plus system effects; new build: system credit only, with the sign set by the build margin. The subgroup’s MWh × MER formula cannot carry a residual rate.
  • GAP. No GHG Protocol text covers CCS in impact accounting; none of the nine tests addresses retrofits or retirement; how a CCS plant’s EAC rate is set, and how generator and buyer Statement 3 claims are kept apart, are unwritten.
