1 · Process

From working group to published standard

The Technical Working Groups recommend, the Independent Standards Board decides content, and the Steering Committee checks that the procedure was followed.

The Standard Development and Revision Procedure (SDRP) sets the sequence: "The development or revision of a standard shall include the following steps, with revisions undergoing these steps on a five-year cycle". Scroll through the steps; the figure highlights each one. SDRP v1.2 §2, p.4

STEP 1 · PREPARATION

The SC decides whether, the ISB approves the plan

"The SC shall be the final arbiter of whether a new standard or a standard revision shall proceed." Then: "Prior to commencement of the work, the SDP and workplan shall be approved by the ISB, as well as any subsequent amendments to either." SDRP v1.2 §3.1.3, §3.2.5, p.5

STEP 2 · TWG WORK

TWGs recommend; they do not decide

"The TWGs shall provide technical input to the Secretariat within their assigned scope, to support in developing proposals and recommendations to the ISB for the content of the applicable draft standard." SDRP v1.2 §4.1.2, p.5

"TWGs do not have the power to take decisions on the final content of standards", and "no indicative votes shall be construed to represent a binding decision." Without consensus, the Secretariat presents the issues and options to the ISB "for evaluation, recommendation and/or final decision." TWG ToR v1 §3.2, §6.2

STEP 3 · ISB DIRECTION AND VOTES

A pulse check is not a vote

Without consensus, ISB decisions need "support or consent of at least two-thirds of all (including those not present) ISB members", and "Abstentions shall be counted as a vote against a proposal." ISB ToR v1.1 §6.2

Most 2025–2026 Scope 2 directions were pulse checks; the formal Scope 2 votes were on 14 July and 22 September 2025. The Secretariat: "a pulse check gives direction and is not a decision". ISB #12 summary ISB #14 summary S2 TWG #23 minutes, p.5

STEP 4 · PUBLIC CONSULTATION

At least 60 days, and comments wait for it

A consultation "consisting of at least one round of sixty (60) days for submission of comments by stakeholders" is required for drafts and major revisions. Outside it, general comments "shall be paused". SDRP v1.2 §4.4.1, §4.3.4, p.6

The next round is a consolidated GHG Protocol public consultation in Q2 2027, when the draft also enters ISO's committee draft (CD) stage. Consolidated SDP, p.16

STEP 5 · RECALIBRATION AND PILOT

Revise, then submit or test

"Based on the comments received during the public consultation period and as necessary, the Secretariat shall, together with the TWGs and ISB, review and revise the draft standard." It then submits the standard for approval or proposes "any further consultation or pilot testing". SDRP v1.2 §4.6, p.7

Pilot testing happens only "If pilot testing has been approved by the ISB" and lasts "at least sixty (60) days". No Scope 2 pilot is scheduled; "If a pilot testing phase is added, the timeline would be extended." SDRP v1.2 §4.7, p.8 Consolidated SDP, p.13

STEP 6 · APPROVAL TO PUBLICATION

The SC checks procedure, not content

The ISB presents the approved draft "for ratification to the SC". The SC ratifies "based on whether the GHG Protocol Standard Development and Revision Procedure was followed throughout the standard-setting process, and whether the resulting standard meets the established Standard Development Plan". A remand needs two-thirds of all SC members. SDRP v1.2 §4.8.1 SC ToR v1 §3.2, §6.2

At the same time the draft goes to ISO/TC 207/SC 7, which approves it if "a two-thirds majority of permanent members" are in favour "and no more than one-quarter of the total number of votes cast are negative." The Co-Hosts then publish. Consolidated SDP, p.16 SDRP v1.2 §4.8.3, p.8

STEP 7 · SIDE CHANNELS

Complaints, observers, coordination

"A dedicated ISB subcommittee, consisting of at least four members including the Chair of the ISB, shall investigate complaints regarding deviations" from the procedure. Complaints Procedure v1.0 §4.1 Observing Entities "do not have any voting rights": "CDP, … EFRAG, … GRI, … ISO, … ISSB and the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)." ISB ToR v1.1 §1.1.4

A joint coordination group, where "GHGP Secretariat and ISO counterparts will meet to facilitate coordination across standards by identifying potential areas of misalignment and raising them to appropriate governance bodies". S2 TWG #22 deck, slide 11

Worked example · who decided the consultation length

On 24 November 2025 an ISB motion to extend the Scope 2 consultation from 60 to 104 days "did not pass" (Yes 5 / No 4 / Abstain 2). The ISB Chair then clarified "that both votes constituted recommendations to the Steering Committee, as the appropriate decision-making body on these topics." The next day "the SC unanimously approved the extension of Scope 2 public consultation to end of January 2026". ISB #16 summary, 24 Nov 2025, pp.1–2 SC minutes Q4 2025, p.2

Inference

Because abstentions count against and two-thirds of all members are needed, 5 votes of 11 could not have passed the motion in any case.

2 · Decision-making criteria

Five criteria, in ranked order

Every body in the process is told to weigh options against the same ranked list. Integrity comes first; feasibility comes last.

The criteria are Annex A of the Governance Overview. The ISB approved them unanimously on 11 September 2024, and the SC "unanimously and provisionally approved" them on 24 September 2024, with the proviso that they "will be revisited in due course as necessary." Governance Overview v1.1, Annex A, pp.6–9 ISB #2 summary, 11 Sep 2024 SC minutes 2024 Q3 The plan for the joint standard says "all governance and advisory bodies of the GHG Protocol shall follow the decision-making criteria and hierarchy". Consolidated SDP, p.12

“The criteria are ranked in a hierarchy to aid in decision making. When options present tradeoffs between criteria, the hierarchy should be generally followed, such that, for example, scientific integrity is not compromised at the expense of other criteria, while aiming to find solutions that meet all criteria.”

Governance Overview v1.1, Annex A · Governance Overview, Annex A, p.6
1A
Scientific integrity
“First, approaches should ensure scientific integrity and validity, adhere to the best applicable science and evidence (including academic literature, modeling or other research) and align with the latest climate science.”
Governance Overview, Annex A
1B
GHG accounting and reporting principles
“Second, approaches should meet the GHG Protocol accounting and reporting principles of accuracy, completeness, consistency, relevance, and transparency. Additional principles should be considered where relevant: conservativeness (for GHG reductions and removals), permanence (for removals), and comparability (TBD, subject to TWG and ISB discussions). Options may present tradeoffs among principles which should be evaluated. Refer to section A.3 for further details.”
Governance Overview, Annex A
2A
Support decision making that drives ambitious global climate action
“Third, approaches should advance the public interest by informing and supporting decision making that drives ambitious actions by private and public sector actors to reduce GHG emissions and increase removals in line with global climate goals.”
Governance Overview, Annex A
2B
Support programs based on GHG Protocol and uses of GHG data
“Fourth, approaches should promote interoperability with key mandatory and voluntary climate disclosure and target setting programs that are based on GHG Protocol standards, where appropriate, while ensuring policy neutrality. Refer to section A.4 for further details.”
Governance Overview, Annex A
3
Feasibility to implement
“Fifth, approaches which meet the above criteria should be feasible to implement, meaning that they are accessible, adoptable, and equitable.”
Governance Overview, Annex A

4 · Two working groups

The AMI TWG sets the frame; the Scope 2 TWG fills in electricity

Scope 2's two methods sit inside the Actions and Market Instruments (AMI) multi-statement framework, which another working group owns.

The division was stated at the October 2026 Scope 2 TWG meeting: "the AMI TWG sets the framework and the purpose of each statement while the Scope 2 TWG develops the electricity-specific details." S2 TWG #23 minutes, p.5 The AMI white paper says "Scope 2-related requirements remain the remit of the Scope 2 TWG." AMI White Paper v3, p.7, fn 5

Read each statement in the sources' own words

Statement 1 · Physical GHG inventory
“The physical GHG inventory as established in the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (Corporate Standard) continues to serve as the foundation of corporate GHG accounting and reporting. It provides comprehensive accounting and disclosure of an organization's annual GHG emissions (and removals, if applicable) resulting from its operations and activities in its value chain across scope 1, scope 2, and scope 3, based on attributional accounting approaches.”
AMI White Paper v3, p.5
ISB framing, Sept 2026 (as relayed)
“Statement 1, to report emissions from use based on physical activities within a company's operations and value chain.”
S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 37

Scope 2 content: the location-based method. The September pulse proposal reads "Companies shall report scope 2 location-based method (LBM)." S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 39 "Beyond the physical GHG inventory being mandatory, no decisions have been made on which statements should be required or optional." AMI White Paper v3, p.35

Statement 2 · Market-based GHG inventory
“The market-based GHG inventory of emissions (and removals, if applicable) is complementary to the physical inventory. It allocates emissions associated with the reporting entity's activities from a common activity pool, based on qualified contractual arrangements for the purchase of goods and services (across scopes). It allows chain of custody models that establish contractual traceability from suppliers to the reporting company. […] The market-based GHG inventory may incorporate the existing scope 2 market-based method and is expected to introduce new approaches for scope 1 and scope 3.”
AMI White Paper v3, p.5
ISB framing, Sept 2026 (as relayed)
“Statement 2, to report emissions from use allocated through qualified contractual arrangements and market instruments.”
S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 37

Scope 2 content: the market-based method, with Method 2 "may" until 203X and only qualifying instruments after it. S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 39 The AMI TWG's July 2026 meeting agreed "Additionality/impact required for Statement 2", with operational details "TBD". AMI TWG #19 deck, slide 20 In the RFI, 87% (326 of 374) said this statement "should be included". AMI RFI Summary, pp.19–20

Statement 3 · GHG impact statement
“The GHG impact statement provides a dedicated, structured statement for reporting on the impacts of actions taken by the reporting company inside and outside its value chain (e.g., emissions avoided, reduced, or removed). […] This statement uses consequential accounting methods that aim to quantify the global change in GHGs in the atmosphere resulting from a given action. This involves estimating the GHG impacts of an action relative to a credible baseline scenario in which the action did not occur.”
AMI White Paper v3, p.6
ISB framing, Sept 2026 (as relayed)
“Statement 3, to define how companies could report consequential GHG impacts of actions not reported in Statements 1 and 2.”
S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 37

Statement 3 "is expected to include quality criteria such as additionality, credible baselines, permanence, mitigate leakage, unique issuance and claiming, monitoring, independent validation and verification, GHG program governance, and sustainable development benefits and safeguards." AMI White Paper v3, p.40 ISB members "unanimously supported developing an electricity sector specific consequential accounting methodology for Statement 3" and "starting this work in Q2 2027". S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 30

Statement 4 · Non-GHG indicators
“This statement provides a standardized reporting structure for various metrics and indicators not expressed in CO2 equivalent (CO2e) but that might influence organizations' decarbonization actions and other decisions.”
AMI White Paper v3, p.6
ISB framing, Sept 2026 (as relayed)
“Key Indicators, to define how Method 2 actions (using non-emissions metrics) or other indicators could be formally recognized after the transition cut-off date.”
S2 TWG #23 deck, slide 37

The AMI TWG "will pause development of Statement 4 for now, with plans to revisit it later." ISB #23 summary, p.2 The AMI deck notes "GHG P will likely provide a 'light' version for Statement 4 in the draft standard". AMI TWG #20 deck, slide 19

Inference

The ISB's landing place for post-transition Method 2 reporting is the least-developed statement in the AMI framework. No source says how this will be resolved for Q2 2027.

Gap

No document says who prevails if the Scope 2 TWG's Quality Criteria and the AMI TWG's Statement 2 criteria conflict. The closest text gives the ISB an advisory role "on how to achieve consistency across TWG outputs and recommend solutions to major technical or policy disagreements and questions within and between TWGs", plus its general power to decide when TWGs cannot reach consensus. ISB ToR v1.1 §3.1.1 v, §3.2

Inference

There is an unresolved tension between optionality and the market-based method. After the RFI, "the newly proposed statements are expected to remain optional for the foreseeable future", and the AMI deck describes GHG Protocol's position as "all likely being optional (apart from keeping Scope 2 MB method mandatory)", while Scope 2's own "[shall/should/may]" is still open. AMI RFI Summary, p.41 AMI TWG #20 deck, slide 24

Inference · where leverage sits

Content is decided by the ISB, which "should set the initial direction" after a consultation and then tasks the TWG with the open questions. GHGP blog, MBM Update, 16 Oct 2026 The 15 December 2026 ISB meeting comes before the Q2 2027 consultation, so that consultation will respond to a draft whose direction is already set. Feedback is "assessed against the decision-making criteria", not counted. S2 TWG #23 minutes, pp.4, 6 Evidence that answers the ISB's own questions, and is framed against integrity, impact and feasibility in that order, matches how decisions are recorded. The ISO national-body vote is a separate point of influence on the joint text.

6 · Position

What to take into a position

Takeaway

TWGs recommend, the ISB decides content, and the Steering Committee checks that the procedure was followed. A pulse check gives direction and is not a decision. On Scope 2, the AMI TWG sets the frame that the Scope 2 TWG fills.

  • Sourced. The criteria are ranked: integrity, then impact, then feasibility, and "the hierarchy should be generally followed". Governance Overview, Annex A
  • Sourced. The AMI TWG agreed in July 2026 that additionality/impact is required for Statement 2, where the Scope 2 market-based method sits. AMI TWG #19 deck, slide 20
  • Inference. Submissions that answer the ISB's open questions with evidence, framed against the ranked criteria, fit how decisions are recorded better than statements of preference.
  • Gap. No rule says which working group prevails if their criteria conflict, and the September 2026 ISB and SC minutes are not yet published. GHGP governance repository, accessed 19 Oct 2026
