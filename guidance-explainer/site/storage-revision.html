Module 3 · Storage

Does legacy storage need new clean energy behind it?

The revision now points to an age limit on eligible resources, and storage gets one undefined phrase. After this page you can say where an age test could attach to a store, what the precedents do, and what is still open.

1 · Status, October 2026

Two market-based methods, one with an age limit

The ISB has set a direction. It is not a decision, and storage has been sent elsewhere.

“Method 1: This approach would incorporate hourly matching, deliverability and incrementality accounting and reporting requirements.”

“Method 2: This approach would maintain existing scope 2 requirements from the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance …”

Scope 2 MBM Update, 16 Oct 2026, p.2 · source
Incrementality, as GHG Protocol now uses it
“… accounting for clean electricity that is matched hourly, delivered on the same grid, and generated by plants built within a set number of years (often called incrementality) makes it more likely that corporate demand supports new clean power.”

Two meanings of “vintage”

In the 2015 guidance, vintage “reflects the date of energy generation from which the contractual instrument is derived. This is different from the age of the facility.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015, §7.5, p.64 In the 2026 table above, “Incrementality/ vintage” labels an asset age limit. Keep the two apart. In 2015, facility age was a disclosure only: companies “should note the year the generation facility that created the certificate/contract was first operational or substantially repowered.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Table 8.1, p.68

“Companies [shall/should/may, language still to be determined] report under the market-based method if data that meets the quality criteria is available. If a company does report scope 2 market-based method emissions:

• Companies may report using Method 2 (current market-based method under 2015 Scope 2 Guidance) until a to-be-determined date,
• Companies should report using Method 1 until a to-be-determined date,
• Prior to that date, companies shall disclose whether they are applying market-based Method 1 or Method 2, and
• After that date, if a company reports scope 2 market-based method emissions, companies shall report using Method 1 if data that meets the quality criteria is available.”

2 · The storage hook

Two words on a slide: “qualifying storage”

The only explicit storage reference in the incrementality design is a list item among open questions for the TWG.

“3. Technical details to implement Method 1 A. Deliverability boundary details B. Incrementality design details”

“Incrementality design details could include: Qualifying operational events within [X] yrs (e.g., new build, repowering, clean conversion, upgrade, or life extension); original off-taker; pro-rata grid-share allocation; qualifying storage”

Open questions referred to the TWG · Scope 2 TWG #23 deck, slide 40 · source
Gap

“Qualifying storage” is undefined. No source states whether a storage asset can itself be a “qualifying operational event”, whether the stored energy must come from qualifying generation, or both. The value of [X] years is not set. Whether “life extension” covers nuclear licence extension or pumped-hydro refurbishment is not said. The TWG #23 minutes also record an open question on “how older resources under time-matched and deliverable contracts would be allocated”, referred to the TWG. TWG #23 minutes, p.5

The deck also lists “generator age” among commonly named key indicators. TWG #23 deck, slide 31

3 · Where the test attaches

The generator, the store, both, or neither

A discharge carries the attributes of the energy that was charged. An age test could look at either asset.

Inference

The stated theory of change, “demand finances new clean capacity where and when it is consumed” TWG #23 minutes, p.6, concerns clean generation capacity. Under pass-through, a discharge certificate conveys the generator's attributes. Applying an age limit to that attribute therefore tests the generator whose energy was stored. The opposite argument is that a new store is itself new “where and when” capacity that moves clean supply into scarce hours. On that logic the store could be the qualifying event, and “qualifying storage” leaves room for it.

“Storage systems need not themselves meet the incrementality requirement, but the EACs that represent electricity stored in such storage systems must meet the incrementality requirement based on the attributes of the generator of such electricity.”

45V final regulations, preamble, 90 FR 2270 · source

The 45V incrementality test: the generating facility has a COD “no more than 36 months before the hydrogen production facility for which the EAC is retired was placed in service”, with uprate, qualifying-State and certain-nuclear pathways. 45V §1.45V-4(d)(3)(i), 90 FR 2318

Inference

A new or uprated store would make discharged energy eligible even if the charged energy came from older generation, subject to cancellation and losses. It rewards new flexibility where clean supply is scarce. It could also let legacy clean energy re-enter Method 1 through the store, so it would need anti-gaming rules. No regulation in the sources takes this approach; the phrase “qualifying storage” leaves room for it.

“… or from renewable electricity from a new storage asset that is located behind the same network connection point as the electrolyser or the installation generating renewable electricity, that has been charged during the same one-hour period in which the electricity under the renewables power purchase agreement has been produced.”

EU 2023/1184, Art. 6 · source

Art. 5(a) requires that the generating installation “came into operation not earlier than 36 months before” the fuel installation. Storage units are excluded from the definition of an installation generating renewable electricity (Art. 2(3)). EU 2023/1184

“All energy has a direct emissions factor associated with generation, and the use of that emissions factor does not depend on whether the generation facility is existing or new, or why the generation has occurred.”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), §11.3, p.90 · source
Inference

Age-neutral pass-through is consistent with 2015 attributional logic. It is inconsistent with the Method 1 direction if an age limit is adopted for generation, because old generation could then be shifted into Method 1 by any store.

4 · Three reader questions

Two separate tests, an overlay, and two legs

The questions people ask about legacy storage come down to two tests. The answers depend on which option the TWG picks.

The SSS overlay (proposed criterion)
“Standard Supply Service emission factors shall convey the direct GHG emission rate associated with resources where there is a traceable and mandatory financial relationship with consumers.” … “A reporting entity may claim, up to its pro rata share, the electricity and associated attributes from SSS resources in the market-based method.”
Examples include “nuclear-support policies applied to state-level electricity supply in the United States” · Scope 2 consultation draft 2025, Criterion 6, p.26
QUESTION (i)

A 50-year-old pumped store beside a nuclear plant

Under 2015 rules (Method 2) no age test exists. Companies that own generation “should treat their grid consumption as though it were supplied by their own generation facilities”, if no claims on that output were sold. The rule was not written for storage. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, §5.6, pp.39–41

Inference

Under Method 1 with I-1, the store's age is irrelevant; the nuclear output must meet the age limit or a qualifying event (“life extension” and “upgrade” are listed candidates). A 50-year-old plant with neither would fail, and the owner would need to charge from qualifying generation. Under I-2 an old store would not qualify unless “qualifying storage” covers storage life extension. Under I-3 it fails both tests; under I-4 it qualifies. If the nuclear output is SSS, only pro-rata claims are possible. Co-location meets leg 1; leg 2 needs the load in the store's deliverable boundary.

Precedents: 45V (generator test; uprate and narrow nuclear pathways) 45V; EU (store would not qualify) EU 2023/1184

Gap

GHG Protocol has not decided which option applies, the value of [X], or what “life extension” covers.

QUESTION (ii)

Does all new storage need incremental clean energy?

45V says yes for the energy and no for the store: stored EACs “must meet the incrementality requirement based on the attributes of the generator”. 45V preamble, 90 FR 2270 The EU requires a “new storage asset” and new generation. EU 2023/1184, Arts 5–6

Inference

Under I-1, yes: a new store's discharge earns Method 1 credit only if the charged energy came from qualifying generation; the store's newness adds nothing. Under I-2, no: the new store can itself be the qualifying event. Under I-3, yes, and the store must also be new. Under I-4, no.

Gap

No GHG Protocol source answers this question.

QUESTION (iii)

Should storage count for its time-shifting benefit, whatever its age?

The inventory is attributional: “Within corporate inventories scope 2 uses attributional methods to assign emissions”. 2025 draft, p.4 Storage and load shifting are pointed at the consequential Statement 3. TWG #23 deck, slide 28

Inference

A store that charges with clean certificates in surplus hours and discharges in scarce hours does move clean attributes into scarce hours. Pass-through records that attributionally, with no consequential estimate. That supports counting backed discharge under hourly QC4, but it does not settle whether the age limit tests the store or the generator. Under I-1 the store's age is moot; the energy's age still matters. “Regardless of age” fits I-4 only if the age limit on charged energy is also dropped. Whether time-shifting lowers system emissions is a consequential question for Statement 3.

Gap

The Statement 3 electricity methodology has not been written; work starts Q2 2027.

“Hourly accounting, market boundaries reflecting power grid realities, and an incrementality standard (SSS or even better: an asset age limit) will very much increase the integrity of scope 2 reporting.”

Environmental Coalition on Standards (ECOS) · NGO, Belgium · Q152 · raw feedback

“Restricting eligible supply based on age could potentially distort the market, increase the cost of clean energy, and discourage companies from signing contracts with older, yet highly functional, carbon-free resources that provide baseload power, these respondents suggested.”

GHG Protocol summary of opposing views · Consultation Summary, 25 Aug 2026, p.93 · source
5 · Options and actors

Five ways to attach the test, and who each one moves

These are options drafted in the research, not GHG Protocol text and not recommendations. Pros and cons reference the decision-making criteria.

The decision-making criteria the pros and cons refer to (verbatim)

“The criteria are ranked in a hierarchy to aid in decision making. When options present tradeoffs between criteria, the hierarchy should be generally followed …”

  • 1A Scientific integrity. “First, approaches should ensure scientific integrity and validity …”
  • 1B GHG accounting and reporting principles. “Second, approaches should meet the GHG Protocol accounting and reporting principles of accuracy, completeness, consistency, relevance, and transparency.”
  • 2A Support decision making that drives ambitious global climate action. Includes: “Accounting approaches should not support or incentivize actions that are contrary to global climate goals.”
  • 2B Support programs based on GHG Protocol and uses of GHG data. “Fourth, approaches should promote interoperability with key mandatory and voluntary climate disclosure and target setting programs that are based on GHG Protocol standards, where appropriate, while ensuring policy neutrality.”
  • 3 Feasibility to implement. “Fifth, approaches which meet the above criteria should be feasible to implement, meaning that they are accessible, adoptable, and equitable.”
GHG Protocol Governance Overview, Annex A, pp.6–8
Option I-1 · Generator only (45V model) · Inference
Rule sketch
Discharge certificates are eligible for Method 1 only if the charged energy's generator meets the age or qualifying-event test. Store age is irrelevant.
Candidate language
Eligibility under [age limit] shall be determined by the generating facility whose attributes were charged into storage; storage systems need not themselves meet [the age limit].
Pros
Coherent with pass-through and QC1; avoids double-gating; consistent with an existing regulatory precedent (2B interoperability).
Cons
No Method 1 credit for new storage that shifts legacy clean energy; may under-reward time-shifting (2A).
Option I-2 · Store as qualifying event · Inference
Rule sketch
A new or uprated store makes discharged energy eligible even if the charged energy is from older generation, subject to cancellation and losses.
Candidate language
Commissioning or repowering of a storage system within [X] years shall be a qualifying operational event for electricity discharged from it, provided …
Pros
Rewards new flexibility capacity “where and when” scarce (2A); recognises time-shifting.
Cons
Could let legacy clean energy re-enter Method 1 via the store, weakening the “new clean capacity” theory; needs anti-gaming rules (for example, no shifting of already-abundant legacy output).
Option I-3 · Both new (EU RFNBO model) · Inference
Rule sketch
The store must be new and co-located; the generation must be new.
Candidate language
… both the storage system and the generating facility shall meet …
Pros
Strictest; simple story.
Cons
Feasibility (3); excludes existing pumped hydro entirely; EU-specific context (hydrogen).
Option I-4 · Neither (age-neutral pass-through) · Inference
Rule sketch
Storage passes attributes through regardless of either asset's age (2015 §11.3 logic).
Pros
Consistent with 2015 attributional logic; feasibility.
Cons
Inconsistent with the Method 1 direction if an age limit is adopted for generation; old generation could be shifted by any store.
Option I-5 · Indicator only · Inference
Rule sketch
Report store and generator age as key indicators (the deck names “generator age” among indicators).
Pros
Transparency; keeps the inventory attributional (the TWG minority view in the 6 Oct minutes).
Cons
Does not change the Method 1 tCO2e figure.
Companion language on ledger mechanics (L1, L2, L3, L5)
Inference · options drafted in the research

L1 Pass-through. Contractual instruments representing electricity discharged from a storage system may be applied to consumption in the hour of discharge, provided that instruments for the electricity charged into the storage system were cancelled for charging in the hour of charging, the quantity applied does not exceed metered discharge, and the storage system is deliverable to the consuming load. Pro: QC2/QC4/QC5 coherence. Con: registry readiness.

L2 Losses. Storage losses (metered charge minus metered discharge over the efficiency period) shall be reported as electricity consumption by the entity operating the storage system [or: by the claimant], using contractual instruments or the residual mix for the hour(s) of charging. Pro: Accuracy, Completeness. Con: allocating losses to the operator burdens merchant storage and may trip exemption thresholds.

L3 Comprehensiveness. All discharge from a registered storage system shall be recorded; discharge not backed by cancelled charging instruments shall carry no attribute claim [or: shall carry the residual mix of the charging hour]. Pro: no cherry-picking. Con: the residual-mix timing rule is undeveloped.

L5 Disclosure. Disclose the storage tag, store location, generator location and age, round-trip efficiency used, and allocation method. Pro: Transparency; comparable with EnergyTag SD-GC fields. Con: reporting burden.

Implications by actor

Developers of new storage · Inference

Under I-1, a new store's certificate value depends on charging from qualifying generation. Co-locating with new generation simplifies both deliverability legs and the age test. Under I-2, the store itself creates eligibility; the revenue opportunity is larger but subject to anti-gaming rules. Registry capability for charge and discharge records is a gating feasibility item: EnergyTag calls storage tracking “still in an early stage of development and implementation”. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, p.25

Generators with legacy fleets · Inference

Method 2 is unaffected until the TBD date. Under Method 1 with an age limit, legacy nuclear, hydro and pumped-storage output is ineligible unless a qualifying event applies (“life extension” and “upgrade” are candidates) or SSS treatment applies. A legacy pumped store can still time-shift qualifying energy under I-1, and so sell a shaping service. Its own age matters only under I-2 or I-3.

Retail suppliers in competitive markets · Inference

Supplier-specific factors must be “calculated based on delivered electricity” (QC6). Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Table 7.1 Storage in a supplier portfolio would need the same pass-through and loss accounting to avoid over-claiming clean hours. SSS rules require pro-rata allocation of mandated resources, which limits concentrating legacy clean attributes through storage products. In Q140, 64.13% of respondents (286 / 446) expected “Shaping/firming or storage products” to drive hourly procurement prices. Consultation raw data

6 · Position

What to take into a position

The question is real, the precedents disagree, and GHG Protocol has not chosen.

Takeaway

Method 1 is proposed to carry an age limit “(shall)”, and storage appears only as the undefined “qualifying storage”. Whether that tests the generator, the store, or both is the open design choice. 45V tests the generator only; the EU tests both.
