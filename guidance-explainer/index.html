Scope 2 Frontier

Carbon capture, electricity storage and impact accounting under the GHG Protocol as it stands, and as the Scope 2 revision is taking shape.
Status as of 19 October 2026

The Independent Standards Board has named an hourly, deliverable and incremental market-based method as the destination for Scope 2. Nothing that would bind a reporter is fixed yet, and none of the GHG Protocol text in force or in draft decides how carbon capture or storage fit. That gap is where positions get shaped now.

The revision now points at Method 1

The September ISB direction is a "hybrid": companies may keep the 2015 method (Method 2) until a year still written as "203X", should move to Method 1 (hourly matching, deliverability, incrementality), and shall disclose which they use. The pulse check was 10 of 10 and the Secretariat says a pulse check "gives direction and is not a decision". Whether the market-based method is required at all is still open. Where the market-based method stands after October 2026

Working groups recommend; the board decides

Technical working groups have no power over final content and their votes are indicative. The ISB decides by two-thirds of all members, with abstentions counting against. The Steering Committee checks that the procedure was followed. On Scope 2, the Actions and Market Instruments group sets the multi-statement frame that the Scope 2 group fills, and no document says who prevails if their criteria conflict. Who actually decides what goes into Scope 2

Fossil CCS is a prevented emission, not a removal

Under the Land Sector and Removals Guidance, captured and stored fossil CO2 is left out of the generator's scope 1 and the power buyer's scope 2 while the geologic-storage requirements are met and monitoring continues; Table 14.1 answers "No" to whether it is a removal. The Guidance never mentions certificates, PPAs, residual mix or the Quality Criteria. LSR Guidance v1.0, Ch. 14

Is captured CO2 from a power plant a removal?

A CCS-backed certificate is permitted by silence

The 2015 Scope 2 Guidance lets certificates come from "any or all generation technology types" and says all generation has an emission-rate attribute, so a CCS plant's power could carry a non-zero-rate instrument. Nothing defines whether that rate is gross or post-capture, and nothing says whether a retrofit counts under the revision's proposed age limit. Registries today issue for any fuel; none was confirmed to issue CCS-specific certificates. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 US 45V final rule

Can a CCS plant's power be sold as a low-carbon attribute?

Storage has no GHG Protocol rule, in force or in draft

Neither the 2015 Guidance nor the 2025 consultation draft sets a storage ledger; storage guidance is assigned to a later informative Guidance v2.0 with no date. The only revision hook is the undefined "qualifying storage" in the incrementality design list. Third-party schemes already cancel certificates on charge and re-issue on discharge net of losses; the US 45V rule tests the generator, not the store; the EU rule tests both. Impact accounting splits plant credit from system credit

The draft "GHG impact statement" would measure the change caused by an action against a baseline, reported apart from the inventories and not netted against them, with additionality proposed as required. In inventory terms a capture retrofit cuts the owner's scope 1. In impact terms, read through the Project Protocol and Grid Guidelines, a retrofit earns an at-plant effect against its own history unless it was about to retire, net of lost output and fuel-chain effects; a new build earns only a system-change credit, which a clean build margin can turn negative. The frame

Three statements, and where each question lands

The Actions and Market Instruments group's draft framework separates a physical inventory, a market-based inventory and a consequential impact statement. Every question on this site sits in one of them, or in the gap between two.

Still open

Where a position can still shape the outcome

Gap · Method 1 parameters

The year 203X, the age limit "[X] yrs" and the deliverability boundaries are not set. The list of "qualifying operational events" includes new build, repowering, clean conversion, upgrade and life extension, undefined.

Oct 2026 update
Gap · Qualifying storage

Whether incrementality tests the generator whose energy is stored, the storage asset, or both. A 50-year-old pumped store beside a nuclear plant qualifies under one reading and not another.

Storage & incrementality
Gap · CCS emission rate

Whether a certificate's "direct" rate is the gross combustion rate or the post-capture rate, and whether certificates are issued per gross or net megawatt-hour after capture energy use.

CCS-backed EACs
Gap · Retrofit as a qualifying event

Whether a capture retrofit counts as "clean conversion" or "upgrade" under the proposed age limit. The US 45V rule treats a retrofit within 36 months as incremental; GHG Protocol materials do not say.

CCS-backed EACs
Gap · One action, two statements

Whether the same change may appear in Statement 1 and Statement 3, and whether a generator and its certificate buyer can both claim the impact. Three options are on the table; none is chosen.

Gap · Who prevails

The Scope 2 group owns the Quality Criteria; the AMI group owns the statement framework and cross-sector criteria. No document says which wins if they conflict.
