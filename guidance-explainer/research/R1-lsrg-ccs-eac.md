## Key definitions (verbatim)

LSR Standard v1.1, glossary (S2):
- Removal: "The transfer of a GHG from the atmosphere to storage within a non-atmospheric pool. Note: Removals can be from biological or technological sinks." (S2 p. 120 / PDF 124)
- Removals (accounting category): "The net transfer of CO2 from the atmosphere to storage within a non-atmospheric pool." (S2 p. 120)
- CO2 capture: "Collection of CO2 from a source for storage within a non-atmospheric pool." (S2 p. 113 / PDF 117)
- Technological sink: "A mechanical or chemical process that removes CO2 from the atmosphere or captures biogenic CO2 from a source, and stores such CO2 or other forms of carbon derived from CO2 removals in non-atmospheric carbon pools." (S2 p. 122 / PDF 126). Note that capture of biogenic CO2 counts as a technological sink. Capture of fossil CO2 is not mentioned.
- Sink: "A biogenic or technological process, activity, or mechanism that removes greenhouse gases from the atmosphere." (S2 p. 121)
- CO2 removals with geologic storage: "Net CO2 removals resulting from annual net increases to carbon stored in geologic carbon pools from carbon derived from biological or technological CO2 sinks." (S2 p. 113)
- Captured biogenic CO2 with geologic storage (accounting subcategory): "Net CO2 removals resulting from annual net increases to carbon stored in geologic carbon pools from carbon derived from biological sinks." (S2 p. 113)
- Technological CO2 removals with geologic storage (accounting subcategory): "Net CO2 removals resulting from annual net increases to carbon stored in geologic carbon pools from carbon derived from technological sinks." (S2 p. 122)
- Geologic carbon pool: "Carbon in geologic formations or inorganic minerals that are not used as products." (S2 p. 115)
- Geologic storage pathway: "The consecutive and interlinked stages associated with the acquisition and storage of carbon in geologic reservoirs." (S2 p. 115)
- Gross CO2 emissions from geologic storage (accounting subcategory): "Gross CO2 emissions from fugitive CO2 emissions or other CO2 losses to the atmosphere from a geologic reservoir containing captured and stored CO2." (S2 p. 116)
- Reversals (accounting category): "An emission from a carbon pool that stores carbon associated with a removal or captured CO2 that was previously reported by the reporting company, and that is no longer within the inventory boundary." (S2 p. 120). Captured CO2 can be reversed, not only removals.
- Scope 1 removals: "Removals for which the reporting company owns or controls both the sink (that transfers CO2 from the atmosphere) and the pool (that stores the CO2 or carbon)." (S2 p. 120)
- Fossil fuel and industrial emissions: "The release of GHGs into the atmosphere from sources other than land emissions. Note: This includes stationary combustion, mobile combustion, fugitive emissions, and process emissions." (S2 p. 115)
- TCDR-based carbon: "Carbon derived from TCDR processes." TCDR-based product: "A good or material that contains TCDR-based carbon during the use stage of the product life cycle." (S2 p. 122)
- Physical traceability: "The ability of a company to identify, track, and collect information on activities (e.g., activity data or GHG emission or removals factors) related to material flows of goods and services in its value chain, across its upstream and downstream processes and products." (S2 p. 119)
- Book and claim (Table 5.2): "A chain of custody model in which the transfer of specified characteristics is not connected to the physical flow of material or products through the supply chain." The table marks it as "No" for "Demonstrates physical traceability?" (S2 p. 25 / PDF 29)

Corporate Standard 2004 (S4):
- Sequestered atmospheric carbon: "Carbon removed from the atmosphere by biological sinks and stored in plant tissue. Sequestered atmospheric carbon does not include GHGs captured through carbon capture and storage." (S4 glossary, p. 101 / PDF 103)

Scope 2 Guidance 2015 (S3):
- Contractual instrument: "Any type of contract between two parties for the sale and purchase of energy bundled with attributes about the energy generation, or for unbundled attribute claims. Markets differ as to what contractual instruments are commonly available or used by companies to purchase energy or claim specific attributes about it, but they can include energy attribute certificates (RECs, GOs, etc), direct contracts (for both low-carbon, renewable or fossil fuel generation), supplier-specific emission rates, and other default emission factors representing the untracked or unclaimed energy and emissions (termed the residual mix) if a company does not have other contractual information that meet the Scope 2 Quality Criteria." (S3 glossary p. 101 / PDF 103. Box 1.1 on p. 9 has near-identical wording.)
- Energy attribute certificate (glossary): "A category of contractual instruments used in the energy sector to convey information about energy generation to other entities involved in the sale, distribution, consumption, or regulation of electricity. This category includes instruments that may go by several different names, including certificates, tags, credits, etc." (S3 p. 102 / PDF 104). Box 1.1 (p. 9) wording: "A category of contractual instrument that represents certain information (or attributes) about the energy generated, but does not represent the energy itself."
- Attribute: "Descriptive or performance characteristics of a particular generation resource. For scope 2 GHG accounting, the GHG emission rate attribute of the energy generation is required to be included in a contractual instrument in order to make a claim." (S3 p. 99 / PDF 101)
- Energy generation facility: "Any technology or device that generates energy for consumer use, including everything from utility-scale fossil fuel power plants to rooftop solar panels." (S3 p. 102. Also Box 1.1, p. 9.)
- Market-based method: "A method to quantify scope 2 GHG emissions based on GHG emissions emitted by the generators from which the reporter contractually purchases electricity bundled with instruments, or unbundled instruments on their own." (S3 p. 104 / PDF 106)
- Residual mix: "The mix of energy generation resources and associated attributes such as GHG emissions in a defined geographic boundary left after contractual instruments have been claimed/retired/canceled. The residual mix can provide an emission factor for companies without contractual instruments to use in a market-based method calculation." (S3 p. 106 / PDF 108)
- Null power: "Energy from which energy attribute certificates or other instruments have been separated and sold off, leaving the underlying power without specific attributes. Also called 'commodity electricity.'" (S3 p. 105 / PDF 107)
- Renewable energy certificate (REC): "A type of energy attribute certificate, used in the U.S. and Australia. In the U.S., a REC is defined as representing the property rights to the generation, environmental, social, and other non-power attributes of renewable electricity generation." (S3 p. 106). Under this definition a REC is renewable-only. "EAC" is the broader category.

Term usage across sources:
- "Removal": LSR (S2) restricts removals to atmospheric CO2 moved into storage by biological or technological sinks. Fossil CCS is "CO2 capture", not a removal (Q1). T7 and T1 use "low-carbon" or "minimal-emitting", never "removal", for CCS power.
- "Capture rate" or "capture percentage": not defined in any GHGP source reviewed. LSR quantifies capture via Equations 14.1 and 14.2 (Q2) and never states a percentage. T7 §9.4 discusses an optional "capture percentage" metric. T1 refers to the "rate of CCS".
- "Incrementality": the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance does not use the word. S6 p. 55 reports "incrementality (defined as new projects in grid modelling studies)". S9 p. 1 describes it as "generated by plants built within a set number of years (often called incrementality)". T1 §1.45V-4(d)(3)(i) defines it operationally by COD or CCS placed-in-service date within 36 months.

## Findings

### Q1. Does LSR treat captured and geologically stored fossil CO2 as a removal?

**Finding 1.1. No. Fossil point-source CCS is a "captured CO2 pathway". It is not a removal. Its effect is that the captured and stored CO2 is not reported as an emission.** [GHGP-inforce]
- S1 §14.1, p. 343 / PDF 347 (same text in S2 p. 77 / PDF 81):
  > "Captured CO2: CO2 collected at a source (preventing an emission) and that continues to be stored in a non-atmospheric carbon pool. In this case, companies do not need to account for those emissions in the relevant scope if they meet the geologic storage requirements, but this is not accounted for and reported as a removal."
  >
  > "Removal: CO2 removed directly from the atmosphere with technologies (e.g., direct air capture), or via biogenic CO2 capture (e.g., bioenergy with carbon capture and storage). These can be accounted for and reported as removals with geologic storage."
- Figure 14.1 (S1 p. 343) has two labelled columns, "Captured fossil CO2 pathway" and "Removal pathways". It shows "GHG capture with geologic storage e.g., point source fossil carbon capture and storage" on one side, and DACCS and BECCS on the other.
- Table 14.1 (S1 p. 350 / PDF 354), column "Does this constitute a removal?": "Captured CO2 pathway / CO2 capture with geologic storage / … / Point source fossil carbon capture and storage (CCS) / No". DACCS, enhanced weathering and BECCS are marked "Yes". The table note says: "Reporting net emissions from captured CO2 pathways and net removals with geologic storage are both also subject to meeting the removal requirements in Chapter 12 and this chapter".
- S1 p. 350:
  > "Fossil carbon capture and storage (CCS): In a fossil CCS pathway, CO2 from fossil fuel and industrial sources is captured on site at a point source. … Captured CO2 pathways with geologic storage where the stored carbon is derived from fossil fuels or other carbon resources that are part of the long-term carbon cycle (e.g., CO2 capture of process emissions from cement production) are not accounted for as CO2 removals. Instead, capture of fossil CO2 from a point source prevents emissions if that CO2 is captured and stored."
- S1 p. 350, on how capture shows up in an inventory:
  > "The impact of CO2 capture is reflected in a GHG inventory by not reporting emissions that have instead been prevented. Captured emissions can result in reduced emissions over time if, in previous years, CO2 was emitted from a company's operations or value chain and in the reporting year, CO2 is instead captured and stored."
- Terminology. LSR's words for fossil CCS are "captured CO2", "CO2 capture with geologic storage" and "prevents emissions". The phrase "avoided emissions" is used elsewhere in LSR for impacts that must stay outside the inventory (e.g., S1 p. 409 / PDF 413: "Any estimates of avoided emissions must not be included in or deducted from the company's scope 1, 2, or 3 inventory"). LSR never labels fossil CCS as "avoided emissions". INFERENCE: pages should not call fossil CCS an "avoided emission" in the LSR sense. LSR treats it as an inventory non-emission, which differs from a project-based avoided-emissions claim.

**Finding 1.2. Distinction from DAC.** [GHGP-inforce]
- S1 p. 351 / PDF 355:
  > "Conceptually, the CO2 capture step is similar to the capture of CO2 from flue gases or other concentrated sources (i.e., in captured CO2 pathways), but DACCS is part of a CO2 removal pathway since it removes CO2 from the atmosphere."

**Finding 1.3. Biogenic capture (BECCS, Bio-CCS) can be a removal. Conditions apply.** [GHGP-inforce]
- S1 p. 351:
  > "Carbon or CO2 stored in geologic reservoirs can be accounted for as CO2 removals if the CO2 originates from biological or technological sinks that are part of the short-term carbon cycle (e.g., CO2 is removed from the atmosphere in the reporting year or is associated with recent biomass growth)."
  >
  > "BECCS systems rely on technologies that capture the biogenic CO2 that is produced when biomass is converted into energy (heat, electricity, or fuel). … BECCS and Bio-CCS may provide a net benefit to the climate if GHG emissions and land carbon leakage (see Chapter 8) over the complete removals and geologic storage pathway … are less than the biogenic carbon captured and stored in geologic reservoirs. The process should also not increase the global dedicated use of land…"
- Requirement 25 (S2 p. 80; S1 p. 346): "If companies account for and report captured biogenic CO2 with geologic storage associated with agricultural products in their GHG inventory, they shall meet the following requirements: … Companies shall account for all life cycle GHG emissions, land use, and land carbon leakage (where required) on lands where the biogenic CO2 or carbon is sourced from … No significant LUC emissions: Companies shall demonstrate that there are no significant land use change emissions attributable to the biogenic materials." The definition of not significant: "If land use change emissions are reported, but less than 5 percent of the sourcing area (i.e., jurisdiction, sourcing region, or LMU) has experienced land use change, this criterion is met." Endnote 3: "This requirement is currently only applicable to geologic storage of biogenic carbon from agricultural products. Requirements for geologic storage of biogenic carbon from forest products are still pending…" (S1 p. 376)
- Fraction rule (S1 p. 373 / PDF 377): "If the source and/or fractional composition of injected CO2 is unknown (i.e., a company does not know if the injected CO2 is derived from biological or technological sinks), Requirement 21.GS is not met and removals cannot be reported."
- Co-firing: S1 does not discuss biomass co-firing with fossil fuel at a capture plant explicitly. INFERENCE: Equations 14.2 and 14.3 split injected CO2 into a fossil/industrial fraction (f_IC) and biogenic/technological fractions (f_IB, f_IT). A co-fired plant would therefore report the fossil share as "CO2 capture with geologic storage" (no emission) and could report only the biogenic share as a removal, subject to Requirement 25. GAP: no explicit co-firing text was found.

**Finding 1.4. Applicability.** [GHGP-inforce]
- Requirement 1 (S2 p. 5 / PDF 9): companies "shall follow the Land Sector and Removals Standard and Guidance if the company has significant land sector activities in its operations or value chain and/or if the company chooses to account for and report CO2 removals or CO2 capture with geologic storage in the current year or has done so in previous years." Table 1.2 lists among the audience: "Companies that store captured fossil CO2, captured biogenic CO2, or CO2 that was technologically removed in geologic reservoirs".
- INFERENCE: from its effective date (01 Jan 2027, S2 p. ii), a fossil generator that wants to exclude captured CO2 from scope 1 is choosing to report "CO2 capture with geologic storage" and is therefore in scope of LSR Chapter 14.
- Corporate Standard 2004: the only capture/CCS text found is the glossary sentence quoted above. GAP: the 2004 Corporate Standard has no rule on scope 1 treatment of captured CO2 (searched "captur", "sequest", "geolog", "carbon storage").

### Q2. How does a capturing company account for captured CO2? Scope 1, storage, monitoring, reversals, roles, CCU and EOR

**Finding 2.1. Chapter 14 shall-statements apply to fossil CO2 capture as well as to removals.** [GHGP-inforce] (S2 pp. 78–81 / PDF 82–85; S1 pp. 344–348 / PDF 348–352)
- Chapter 14 intro (S1 p. 344): "CO2 removals with geologic storage and CO2 capture with geologic storage are subject to the CO2 removal requirements provided in Chapter 12." S1 p. 349 adds: "a company in a value chain with fossil carbon capture and storage must satisfy the same requirements to not report fossil emissions of CO2 that is captured and prevented at a source and ultimately stored in geologic reservoirs."
- REQUIREMENT 19.GS, "Removals and CO2 capture with geologic storage (GS) accounting":
  > "If companies account for and report removals with geologic storage or CO2 capture with geologic storage in their GHG inventory, they shall meet the following requirements: • Life cycle GHG emissions: Companies shall account for all life cycle GHG emissions that occur throughout the geologic storage pathway (i.e., cradle to grave). • This includes GHG emissions, land use, and land carbon leakage (where required) from the product life cycle(s) associated with the stored CO2 or carbon, and report them in the corresponding reporting category in scope 1, scope 2, and/or scope 3. • Enhanced oil and gas recovery: For geologic storage pathways with enhanced oil and gas recovery, companies shall account for all downstream GHG emissions associated with the extraction, processing, transportation, distribution, storage, and use (i.e., combustion) of oil, natural gas, or other hydrocarbons produced from the geologic reservoir and report such emissions in scope 1, scope 2, and/or scope 3."
  - The "Operational boundary" bullet of 19.GS (contractual agreement; "a single tonne of CO2 removal with geologic storage shall not be reported by more than one entity under scope 1") is written for "scope 1 net removals with geologic storage". Its text addresses removals, not captured fossil CO2. S1 Endnote 1 to 19.GS: "This text includes elements that may change to align with the resolution on forest carbon accounting in future versions of this Standard."
- REQUIREMENT 20.GS, "Scope 3 geologic storage traceability":
  > "If companies account for and report scope 3 removals with geologic storage or CO2 capture with geologic storage in their GHG inventory, they shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if they have physical traceability (following Requirement 8) to the entity (or entities) capturing the CO2 received at the injection site or geologic storage hub system, and the entity (or entities) operating the CO2 injection site(s) and geologic storage reservoir(s)."
- REQUIREMENT 21.GS, "Data quality for geologic storage": "…shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if net CO2 removals with geologic storage, CO2 capture with geologic storage, and life cycle GHG emissions for the CO2 stored in the geologic reservoir(s) are accounted for using data specific to the CO2 injection site(s), geologic storage reservoir(s), and CO2 inputs into the geologic storage reservoir(s). • Uncertainty: Companies shall … only if they provide quantitative uncertainty estimates."
- REQUIREMENT 23.GS, "Permanence for geologic storage":
  > "• Ongoing storage monitoring: Companies shall account for and report net removals with geologic storage (or no emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage) only if ongoing storage monitoring is documented in a monitoring plan to ensure carbon remains stored in geologic reservoirs and the company can detect losses of stored carbon from relevant geologic carbon pools. • Losses of stored carbon: Companies shall account for net geologic carbon stock losses of previously reported net removals or captured CO2 with geologic storage in the year the losses occur, as either: • Fossil fuel and industrial emissions, if the carbon pools are part of the GHG inventory boundary in the reporting year; or • Reversals from geologic storage, if the carbon pools are no longer in the GHG inventory boundary in the reporting year. **Finding 2.2. Who reports what for a fossil plant with capture.** [GHGP-inforce, Guidance text: "must" and imperative wording, not Standard shall-statements] (S1 §14.4.1.4, pp. 359–360 / PDF 363–364)

- Hub allocation (S1 p. 360): emissions "should" be allocated "based on physical allocation of the volume of CO2 supplied".

**Finding 2.3. Quantification.** [GHGP-inforce] (S1 §14.5.3, p. 371 / PDF 375. The equation images were checked visually by rendering the page.)
- "Companies that report no CO2 emissions in scope 1, scope 2, or scope 3 for CO2 that is captured and stored in geologic reservoirs must estimate any fugitive emissions of captured CO2 following Equation 14.1 and track any losses of stored carbon using Equation 14.2."
- Equation 14.1: `E_CAPCO2 = CO2_CAP − CO2_STOR`, which is captured CO2 minus CO2 capture with geologic storage.
- Equation 14.2: `CO2_STOR = [CO2_INJ − CO2_L − CO2_F] × f_IC`. Here CO2_INJ is injected CO2, CO2_L is EOR losses (Eq. 14.5), CO2_F is fugitive CO2 from the reservoir (Eq. 14.6), and f_IC is the "Fraction of injected CO2 from captured fossil fuel and industrial CO2".
- "CO2 capture with geologic storage should only be reported for the portion of captured CO2 injected into a geologic reservoir that remains stored. If this value is negative for a given year, it should be reported as losses of stored carbon".
- Equation 14.5 (EOR losses) uses C_PROD (carbon produced), CO2_VENT, CO2_R (reinjected) and 44/12 (S1 p. 374). Equations 14.6 and 14.7 (fugitive) use measured CO2 at the leakage site, natural fluctuation, an invariant baseline, flux per hectare, and leak area (S1 pp. 375–376).
- Data checklist, Table 14.6 (S1 p. 370): "Injected CO2 into the geologic reservoir in the reporting year; Fraction of injected CO2 from captured fossil fuel and industrial CO2; Losses of CO2 from enhanced oil recovery in the reporting year, if applicable; Fugitive CO2 from the geologic reservoir in the reporting year".
- GAP: LSR gives no default or typical capture rate. The scope 1 effect follows from metered captured and stored quantities.

**Finding 2.4. Permanence guidance.** [GHGP-inforce] (S1 pp. 365–368)
- The monitoring obligation names scope 2 explicitly (S1 p. 365): "No emissions reporting for CO2 capture with geologic storage: Ongoing storage monitoring of geologic reservoirs in the value chain of the reporting company, where the company previously did not report scope 1, scope 2, or scope 3 emissions associated with CO2 capture with geologic storage."
- S1 p. 367: "Where such characterization and operations are done properly, this process can result in zero CO2 losses from geologic storage (e.g., defined as less than 1 percent of the total injected mass lost over 100 to 1000 years)." The source cited is National Academies 2019 (endnote 8).
- Transfer of liability (S1 p. 366): "If there is a transfer of liabilities associated with geologically stored CO2 to the state or other jurisdiction, and the geologic storage operator demonstrates that storage is permanent and without risks of losses, the date and condition of the transfer of liabilities should be clarified, and post-closure monitoring may follow regulatory guidance."

**Finding 2.5. CCU, products and EOR. Utilization is not geologic storage. Fossil carbon in products is a temporary non-emission.** [GHGP-inforce]
- S1 p. 351 / PDF 355:
  > "Captured CO2 emissions with product storage or use as an input for other production purposes (e.g., greenhouse fertilization) do not represent a captured CO2 pathway with geologic storage (i.e., does not include permanent storage in geologic reservoirs). Carbon captured and stored in products represents temporary storage, followed by re-release to the atmosphere at the end of the product lifecycle. Reporting product carbon storage is optional in this Standard. Companies that choose to report product carbon storage must satisfy the requirements in Chapter 15."
- Chapter 15 (S1 p. 384 / PDF 388), "Captured GHG and storage pathway":
  > "For example, CO2 emissions from cement production … can be captured … and sequestered into concrete as a final product. … captured GHGs and their storage in products prevents the release of CO2 emissions to the atmosphere for a period of time and is therefore reflected in a GHG inventory as a non-emission for that period of time. If companies cannot establish the source of captured CO2 emissions (i.e., fossil, biogenic, or TCDR-based carbon), companies should conservatively account for the captured CO2 as fossil CO2."
- Product carbon storage, the optional separate category, covers only biogenic or TCDR-based carbon. Requirement 24 (S1 p. 379): "The net product carbon stock change shall only include biogenic and/or TCDR-based carbon in agricultural or TCDR-based products sold by the reporting company…". S1 p. 385: "only applicable to biogenic and TCDR-based carbon".
- TCDR-based products (Requirement 18, S2 p. 59; S1 §11.4.3, p. 290 / PDF 294) are products whose carbon is "technologically removed CO2" (DAC or captured biogenic). If origin cannot be shown, emissions go to "fossil fuel and industrial emissions". Example from S1: "a company that purchases a concrete mix that is cured with direct-air-captured CO2 must establish that the CO2 used in the curing process is technologically removed…". INFERENCE: fossil-CCU products are not TCDR-based products.
- EOR: geologic storage with EOR is permitted, subject to the 19.GS cradle-to-grave rule above. Box 14.1 (S1 p. 353): "Where the net geologic carbon stock change is positive and greater than the life cycle GHG emissions of the geologic storage with EOR pathway … the EOR can provide climate benefits. Where the net geologic carbon stock change is negative, it leads to net CO2 emissions from geologic storage." S1 p. 357 points to Chapter 16 consequential methods for "the displacement of non-EOR oil and gas production".

### Q4. Under the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance, can an EAC represent CCS-equipped fossil generation, and with what emission factor?

**Finding 4.1. EACs are not limited to renewables. Fossil and "all-generation" instruments are expressly contemplated.** [GHGP-inforce]
- S3 §10.2, p. 80 / PDF 82: "Historically, most certificates for policies or consumer programs have been generated from renewable energy resources, driven by demand for these resources in particular, but depending on their intended purpose or usage certificates can be generated from any or all generation technology types. For example, all-generation certificate tracking exists in the northeast U.S."
- S3 p. 80: "All energy generation has a GHG emission rate attribute, even if that attribute is 'zero emissions/MWh' at the point of generation."
- S3 p. 90 / PDF 92: "The market-based method for scope 2 accounting applies to all energy generation in a defined grid, not just 'low-carbon' or renewable energy from projects supported by a specific company's financial support. It concerns the larger allocation process of all energy emissions across all end users. All energy has a direct emissions factor associated with generation, and the use of that emissions factor does not depend on whether the generation facility is existing or new, or why the generation has occurred."
- S3 §7.4.2, p. 63 / PDF 65: "the market-based method should also include other contractual instruments representing fossil fuel or mixed-resource emission factors as well."
- Table 6.3 (S3 p. 48 / PDF 50) lists, under contracts: "In the U.S., contracts for electricity from specified nonrenewable sources like coal in regions other than NEPOOL and PJM". Footnote, S3 p. 31: "Only the NEPOOL and PJM regions of the U.S. use all generation certificate tracking."
- "Low-carbon" wording: the 2015 text uses "low-carbon" descriptively (e.g., contractual instruments "for both low-carbon, renewable, or fossil fuel generation", Box 1.1 p. 9), but sets no "low-carbon" eligibility class. "Nuclear" appears as an attribute example ("nuclear waste quantities", p. 79), in risk discussion, and in "most renewable or nuclear technology would report 'zero' emissions from this generation" (p. 37). GAP: no explicit technology eligibility list exists. The 2015 Guidance is "policy-neutral" (p. 8 summary: "These Scope 2 Quality Criteria are policy-neutral").
- INFERENCE: nothing in the 2015 text bars an EAC from a CCS-equipped fossil plant. Such an EAC would be an instrument with a non-zero emission rate. A "REC", as the 2015 glossary defines it, is renewable-only, so the CCS instrument would be a generic EAC or a fossil-source contract.

**Finding 4.2. The emission factor that travels with the instrument.** [GHGP-inforce]
- QC1 (Table 7.1, S3 p. 60 / PDF 62): the instrument shall "Convey the direct GHG emission rate attribute associated with the unit of electricity produced."
- Table 6.3 header (S3 p. 48): "Data forms listed here should convey combustion-only (direct) GHG emission rates, expressed in metric tons per MWh or kWh."
- §6.11 (S3 p. 54): "To ensure this, instruments shall include the GHG emission rate attribute."
- §4.2 (S3 p. 27): the methods "use generation-only emission factors (e.g. emissions assessed at the point of energy generation)… The emission factors do not include T&D losses or upstream life-cycle emissions associated with the technology or fuel used in generation. Instead, these other categories of upstream emissions should be quantified and reported in scope 3, category 3".
- When the rate is not on the certificate (S3 §7.5, p. 64 / PDF 66): "If the attribute emission rate itself is not specified and the technology is not zero emissions, the reporting organization should seek from the generating entity a specific emission rate from that generation facility. Otherwise, a default factor from IPCC or other government publications may be used and disclosed."
- Biofuel analogue (S3 p. 64): "Biofuel generation facilities producing certificates should specify the CO2, CH4, and N2O emissions produced at the point of generation. The scope 2 reporter reports the CH4 and N2O emissions in scope 2, while the CO2 from biofuel is reported separately from the scopes."
- On-site generation (Table 5.2, S3 p. 40): "Scope 1 reporting shall still reflect any emissions from the generator."
- GAP: the 2015 text does not say whether a CCS plant's "direct" or "combustion-only" rate means gross combustion CO2 or CO2 emitted after capture.
- INFERENCE: the Corporate Standard defines emissions as releases, and LSR (Finding 2.2) excludes captured and stored CO2 from the generator's scope 1. A "direct" rate consistent with LSR would therefore be the post-capture emitted rate, meaning uncaptured stack CO2 plus capture-site fugitive emissions per MWh. That rate is conditioned on the LSR GS requirements being met (storage monitoring, site data, reversals).
- The 2015 note that, absent a specified rate for a non-zero technology, a default IPCC or government factor "may be used" would, for CCS, presumably mean an unabated fossil factor. INFERENCE only.
- Net generation: GAP. The 2015 Guidance is silent on whether a CCS EAC is issued per gross or per net MWh, after the parasitic load of capture and compression. T4 requires deducting auxiliary energy (Q6).

### Q6. Market instruments today for low-carbon non-renewable attributes (third-party tier)

**Finding 6.1. US 45V final rule (T1): EACs may represent any technology, including CCS power, with a non-zero rate. A CCS retrofit pathway exists for incrementality.** [third-party]
- EAC definition, §1.45V-4(d)(2)(ii), 90 FR 2316: "The term energy attribute certificate (EAC) means a tradeable contractual instrument, issued through a qualified EAC registry or accounting system …, that represents the energy attributes of a specific unit of energy produced. An EAC may be traded with or separately from the underlying energy it represents. … Renewable energy certificates (RECs) and other similar energy certificates issued through a registry or accounting system are forms of EACs."
- Eligible EAC contents, §1.45V-4(d)(2)(iii), 90 FR 2316: "(A) A description of the facility, including the technology and feedstock used to generate the electricity; … (C) The COD of the facility …; (D) For electricity that is generated before January 1, 2030, the calendar year in which such electricity was generated; (E) For electricity that is generated after December 31, 2029, the date and hour …; (F) Other attributes required by 45VH2-GREET or in the determination of a PER to accurately determine the emissions associated with such electricity; (G) For electricity generating sources that use carbon capture equipment, the placed in service date of such equipment; and (H) The project identification number or assigned identifier."
- Incrementality, §1.45V-4(d)(3)(i)(A), 90 FR 2318: "…has a COD that is no more than 36 months before the hydrogen production facility for which the EAC is retired was placed in service, or, if the electricity represented by the EAC is produced by an electricity generating facility that uses carbon capture and sequestration (CCS) technology, such technology has a placed in service date that is no more than 36 months before the hydrogen production facility for which the EAC is retired was placed in service."
- Preamble rationale, 90 FR 2259: "These final regulations adopt the CCS retrofit rule because an electricity-generating facility retrofit with carbon capture equipment may be considered a new source of lower-carbon supply. … EACs from plants retrofitted with new carbon capture equipment will not have a zero emissions rate, and this information would need to be reflected accordingly in 45VH2-GREET as part of the GHG emissions rate calculation." Also: "these final regulations do not adopt a rule that CCS retrofits mandated by law are not incremental."
- Storage condition, §1.45V-4(e), 90 FR 2320: CCS "may be taken into account only if the carbon capture occurs … in the production of electricity, fuel, or feedstock that is used by such facility to produce hydrogen and is captured and disposed of in secure geological storage, pursuant to section 45Q(f)(2) … or utilized in a manner described in section 45Q(f)(5)".
- Non-zero sources, 90 FR 2277–2278: "the Treasury Department and the IRS intend to allow the use of EACs with respect to sources of electricity other than zero GHG-emitting electricity. … 45VH2-GREET currently includes certain minimal-emitting electricity source options, including allowing hydrogen production facilities to account for electricity generation using CCS… Foreground data parameters relevant to electricity sources (for example, the amount of CCS) must be verified by a third-party verifier." On 90 FR 2275, CCS integrated with an NGCC is given as a verifiable foreground parameter: "One example is the rate of CCS integrated with a natural gas combined cycle turbine used for power generation."
- Caveats: 45V is a tax credit for hydrogen production, not a GHG inventory standard. It uses lifecycle (well-to-gate) accounting via 45VH2-GREET, which differs from the scope 2 "direct" emission rate. GAP: later statutory changes to 45V (2025 legislation) were not verified against the statute.

**Finding 6.2. EU Guarantees of Origin (T2): Member States may issue GOs for non-renewable energy. The mandatory GO fields do not include a CO2 emission rate.** [third-party]
- RED Art. 19(2) (consolidated 16.07.2024, p. 45): "Member States may arrange for guarantees of origin to be issued for energy from non-renewable sources. … No more than one guarantee of origin shall be issued in respect of each unit of energy produced."
- Art. 19(7), p. 47: "A guarantee of origin shall specify at least: (a) the energy source from which the energy was produced and the start and end dates of production … (c) the identity, location, type and capacity of the installation …; (d) whether the installation has benefited from investment support …; (e) the date on which the installation became operational; and (f) the date and country of issue and a unique identification number." CO2 is not in the minimum list.
- Art. 19(8), p. 48: "Where Member States have arranged to have guarantees of origin for other types of energy, suppliers shall use for disclosure the same type of guarantees of origin as the energy supplied."
- AIB (T3): "This implies Guarantees of Origin for renewable source electricity and cogeneration, and disclosure certificates for fossil and nuclear source electricity, as required by national legislation." Also: "Certificates can also be used to disclose other forms of electricity, including that which originates from nuclear and fossil fuels. … This is in place in some countries (Austria, Sweden, Switzerland), under national law."
- INFERENCE: for 2015 QC1, a GO or disclosure certificate from a CCS plant would need a GHG emission rate supplied on or with it. Art. 19(7) does not require one.
- GAP: no EU "low-carbon" electricity GO specific to CCS was found. Directive 2024/1788, on low-carbon gases, was not retrieved.

**Finding 6.3. EnergyTag GC Scheme Standard v2 (T4) is technology-agnostic. The production emissions factor is optional. Auxiliary load is deducted.** [third-party]

**Finding 6.6. The NorthBridge "CCS EAC" proposal (T7, Oct 2025).** [third-party, proposal]
- Structure (p. 5): "One EAC is issued for each megawatt-hour of generation. The EAC is minted with an associated emission rate measured in pounds of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) per megawatt-hour. When retired, the attribute holder may claim the pounds of CO2e listed on the attribute in its market-based Scope 2 inventory."
- Boundary (p. 6): "The emission rate calculation methodology includes emissions from electricity generation, CO2 capture, CO2 transport, and CO2 storage". Eq. 1 (p. 30): (CO2e Emissions G&C + T + S) / Net MWh.
- Self-acknowledged tension with GHGP (p. 18): "If future Greenhouse Gas Protocol guidance determines that some or all of the capture, transport, and storage emissions for purchased electricity should be included in an inventory other than Scope 2, then the emissions conveyed on a CCS EAC should be allocated between the appropriate scopes." Footnote 15: "the EAC may bear a Scope 2 emission rate and a Scope 3 emission rate."
- INFERENCE: LSR Guidance (Finding 3.1) already assigns transport, storage and off-site fugitive emissions to the buyer's scope 3 category 3, not scope 2.
- Double counting (p. 18–19): "Registering Entities may not pursue carbon credits (or other environmental attributes) for the emissions captured and stored within the project boundary that are included in the calculation methodology and reflected in an EAC." Also: "The calculation of emission rates for grid power or the residual electricity mix should remove the generation and emissions from electric generating units receiving CCS EACs." And: "Pursuing federal tax credits and/or avoiding state carbon taxes or penalties in addition to generating EACs under this methodology is not considered double counting."
- Post-capture pathways (p. 21): sequestration, EOR, EGR and other utilization. "Registries may elect to focus on permanent sequestration applications in Class VI wells … Registries may prefer not to place any restrictions on the end use". The pathway is to be disclosed on the EAC.
- Additionality (p. 45): "This standard does not require imposing additionality requirements on projects that apply for EACs." CFE scoring is fractional: (1 − rate/unabated) × 100% (Eq. 11).
- Capture percentage (p. 46): optional and informational. "Michigan's" clean energy standard is cited as one that recognizes CCS at a set capture rate. The rate is not given in T7. GAP.
- Status: proposal with an input period (ccseac.com). T10 (Utility Dive, 2025-12-12) describes it as a standard for registries. No registry adoption was found. GAP.

## Open questions / gaps

1. GAP: no GHGP text says whether the CCS-plant emission rate used for scope 2 is the gross combustion rate or the post-capture emitted rate. LSR's "do not report scope 2 emissions for the CO2 that is captured and stored" points to post-capture (INFERENCE). The 2015 Scope 2 Guidance never mentions capture.
2. GAP: LSR does not say whether its scope 2 buyer treatment applies under location-based, market-based or both. It does not mention EACs, PPAs, residual mix or the Quality Criteria.
3. GAP: Requirement 20.GS demands physical traceability for scope 3 only. LSR is silent on the scope 2 analogue. Requirement 8 says book-and-claim cannot demonstrate physical traceability. Whether an unbundled CCS EAC can carry the LSR "no emissions" treatment into a buyer's scope 2 is unresolved.
4. GAP: S1 p. 365 says buyers who did not report scope 2 emissions for captured CO2 need "ongoing storage monitoring of geologic reservoirs in the value chain". No mechanism is given for how an EAC buyer satisfies this, or reports reversals of CO2 capture (as defined) years later.
5. GAP: allocation of capture-plant parasitic load (gross versus net MWh) is not addressed by GHGP. T4 requires deducting auxiliaries. T7 uses Net MWh.
6. GAP: the 2015 Table 8.1 "substantially repowered" is undefined. It is unclear whether a CCS retrofit resets facility age for disclosure or for a future Method 1 age limit. 45V treats the CCS placed-in-service date as the incrementality date (T1). GHGP has not addressed this. The S11 design list ("clean conversion, upgrade") is only suggestive (INFERENCE).
7. GAP: interaction with carbon credits for the same captured tonnes (ACR or VCS CCS methodologies, per T7) and with 45Q. The SDP flags credit/EAC interaction only for "carbon-free generation facilities".
8. GAP: how the proposed "fossil-based" fallback factor (S5 p. 28) treats CCS-equipped fossil generation.
9. GAP: CCU and EOR for power-plant CO2. LSR allows EOR with cradle-to-grave accounting of produced hydrocarbons (19.GS). It is unclear how that downstream burden would travel with an EAC. T7 says the buyer "retains these obligations".
10. GAP: no live registry issuance of CCS-specific certificates was confirmed. WREGIS, NAR, NEPOOL-GIS and I-REC were not checked. EU low-carbon GO legislation (Dir. 2024/1788) was not retrieved.
11. GAP: biomass co-firing at a capture plant is not explicitly addressed in LSR text (see the INFERENCE in Finding 1.3).
12. Not done: comparison of LSR Standard v1.0 (Jan 2026) against v1.1. The v1.1 change summary lists no Chapter 14 changes.
