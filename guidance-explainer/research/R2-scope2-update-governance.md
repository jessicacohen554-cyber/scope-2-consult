## Key definitions (verbatim, cited)

### Market-based method (MBM)
- 2015 (in force): "A method to quantify scope 2 GHG emissions based on GHG emissions emitted by the generators from which the reporter contractually purchases electricity bundled with instruments, or unbundled instruments on their own." — S61, Glossary, PDF p.106 (printed p.104). [GHGP-inforce]
  - Table 4.1 variant: "A method to quantify the scope 2 GHG emissions of a reporter based on GHG emissions emitted by the generators from which the reporter contractually purchases electricity bundled with contractual instruments, or contractual instruments on their own" — S61, Table 4.1, PDF p.28 (printed p.26). [GHGP-inforce]
- 2025 consultation proposal (question text, not final text): "the proposal to clarify the MBM definition to retain its existing basis, quantifying scope 2 from contractually purchased electricity via contractual instruments, while specifying temporal correlation and deliverability when matching instruments to consumption" — S53, Q20, p.8. [GHGP-revision]
- 2026: the ISB's September 2026 framing (as reported by the Secretariat): "The MBM is a value-chain inventory metric, and there should be a plausible physical connection between the generated electricity and load for an EAC to be claimed within the value chain. Other EAC procurement is valid, but should be reported separately, not within the value chain." — S04, slide 37. [GHGP-revision]
- "Method 1" / "Method 2" (2026 working terms; names explicitly still open — S04 slide 40 item 1 "Names or ways to refer to each MBM"):
  > "Method 1: This approach would incorporate hourly matching, deliverability and incrementality accounting and reporting requirements."
  > "Method 2: This approach would maintain existing scope 2 requirements from the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance, allowing organizations to continue their current procurement strategies and addressing stakeholder concerns regarding feasibility and market participation." — S02 p.2; identical in S01.
  - Slide 34 of S04 ("Method 1 and Method 2 as presented for the September ISB meeting"): Method 1 = "Hourly matching, deliverability, incrementality"; Temporal "Hourly (shall)"; Geographic "Deliverability (shall) / Market boundaries aligned with grid"; Incrementality/vintage "Age limit on eligible resources for voluntary claims (shall)"; Transition "Transition/phase-in period until [TBD]"; "No legacy contract provision for method 1"; feasibility "Load profiles (may), transition period"; unit "tCO2e". Method 2 ("based on 2015 Guidance MBM") = "Annual matching, existing market boundaries"; Temporal "Annual (or monthly)"; "Existing market boundaries"; incrementality "No requirement"; "Transition/sunset period until [TBD]"; "Legacy contracts continue to be reflected in method 2"; unit "TBD [tCO2e or %MWh matching, volume of EACs, or other metric after]". [GHGP-revision]

### Location-based method (LBM)
- 2015: "A method to quantify scope 2 GHG emissions based on average energy generation emission factors for defined locations, including local, subnational, or national boundaries." — S61, Glossary, PDF p.106 (printed p.104). [GHGP-inforce]
- 2025 proposal (question text): "the proposal to clarify the LBM definition to reflect scope 2 emissions from generation physically delivered at the times and locations of consumption, with imports included in LBM emission factor calculations where applicable" — S53, Q19, p.8. [GHGP-revision]
- 2026 ISB pulse check item 1: "Companies shall report scope 2 location-based method (LBM)." — S04, slide 39. In the AMI framework, Scope 2 LBM sits in Statement 1 (Physical GHG inventory) — S27 Figure ES-1 p.4; S04 slide 27. [GHGP-revision]

### Hourly matching (temporal correlation, Quality Criterion 4)
- 2025 proposed text: "Criterion 4. Temporal correlation. All contractual instruments used in the market-based method for scope 2 accounting shall be issued and redeemed for the same hour as the energy consumption to which the instrument is applied, except in certain cases listed in [Exemption to Quality Criteria 4]. Temporal correlation reflects the simultaneity of the energy supply from which an emission factor is derived and the consumption to which the factor is applied." — S53 p.20. [GHGP-revision]
- 2015 baseline as characterized by GHG Protocol in 2025: "certificates should match the time of consumption 'as closely as possible'" — S54 §4.1. Exec summary: the proposal would "move from requiring contractual instruments be matched 'as close as possible' to the electricity an organization consumes to require hourly matching of contractual instruments for large organizations. Organizations under a threshold would be exempt and able to continue annual matching." — S51 p.4.
- 2026: Method 1 "Hourly (shall)" — S04 slide 34. ISB pulse (Sept 2026) removes thresholds from Method 1: "thresholds sit outside the Method 1 framework the ISB supported" — S03 p.5.
- ISO WG4 (WD2) differs: "Monthly: mandatory (shall)… Annual : mandatory (shall). • Hourly: optional (non consensus about should/may)." plus "Anti-Banking Provision (New)… Contractual instruments shall not be rolled over between annual reporting periods" — S04 slide 63; "Hourly matching remains optional due to a lack of consensus (10 votes for 'should', 15 for 'may')" — S04 slide 64. [GHGP-revision; ISO WG4 content presented in GHGP deck]

### Deliverability (market boundaries, Quality Criterion 5)
- 2025 proposal: "redefines the market boundary to reflect deliverability, which means that electricity from a generator could plausibly be part of the mix serving the reporting entity through an electrically connected grid." Proposed text: "Criterion 5. Market Boundaries and alternate methodology for deliverability: All contractual instruments used in the market-based method shall be sourced from generation that is deemed deliverable to the consuming load. Deliverability shall be demonstrated using one of the approved methodologies listed in the table Proposed methodologies for demonstrating deliverability." — S53 p.23.
- TWG indicative vote wording (June 2025): "Deliverability means it is plausible that electricity generated by a supplier could be included in the mix powering a reporting entity at the time of consumption." — S56 p.3.
- 2026 ISB framing: "plausible physical connection between the generated electricity and load" — S04 slide 37; open design items: "Responses to consultation feedback on how to demonstrate plausible deliverability. For example, principles for boundaries that reflect how the grid physically operates, defined market boundaries, guidance on selecting boundaries, and alternative methods for cross-border delivery." — S04 slide 40.
- **Different usage in AMI**: in the AMI Statement 2 claims subgroup, "Deliverability used to broadly refer to whether there is a possibility that a specific attribute could be included in the commodity or service that a reporter receives" — S32 slide 45 (numbered 46 in footer). [GHGP-revision]
- ISO WG4: "'Deliverability' concept removed." Replaced by a "principle-based framework for determining market boundaries" — S04 slide 67.

### Incrementality / additionality
- **Incrementality (2026 usage)**: "generated by plants built within a set number of years (often called incrementality)" — S02 p.1 (Theory of change 1). Method 1: "Age limit on eligible resources for voluntary claims (shall)" — S04 slide 34. Open design options: "Incrementality design details could [include]: Qualifying operational events within [X] yrs (e.g., new build, repowering, clean conversion, upgrade, or life extension); original off-taker; pro-rata grid-share allocation; qualifying storage" — S04 slide 40.
- Consultation-summary usage: "an incrementality requirement, such as allowing companies to make voluntary clean electricity claims only when those claims are linked to newer clean energy projects" — S51 p.6; "Without an incrementality requirement (i.e., only newer resources)" — S52 Table 18 p.48 and Table 26 p.74.
- 2025 TWG usage: "Incrementality criteria – Voluntary procurements can only be counted if they meet criteria that indicates the procurement contributes to incremental CFE generation (e.g., facility age, original off taker, subsidy limits)." distinguished from "Causality tests – Voluntary procurements can only be counted if they meet criteria that indicates proof of causality (e.g., regulatory, financial, and timing-based tests, positive lists)." — S57 deck slide 19 (Mar 19 2025).
- **Additionality** (AMI white paper lists 8 candidate definitions, no single one adopted), e.g. "1. A criterion often applied to GHG projects stipulating that project-based GHG reductions should only be quantified if the project activity 'would not have happened anyway'…" (GHG Protocol for Project Accounting) and "8. 'Additionality, as a concept, describes the relationship between cause and effect. For any cause and effect, the effect can be described as being additional if it would not have occurred in the absence of the cause.' (ISO 14064-2)" — S27 §5.4 pp.20–21.
- MIM (2025 consequential subgroup) additionality: "consisting of a regulatory test, a timing test, and either a positive list test or a financial additionality test" — S56 Q13 p.5.
- AMI 2026 terminology is unsettled: "Terminology pending: possible names include additionality, impact, causality, system level impact, real economy impact or market transformation impact." — S33 slide 22.

### Standard Supply Service (SSS)
- 2025 proposed text: "Criterion 6. Standard Supply Service: Standard Supply Service emission factors shall convey the direct GHG emission rate associated with resources where there is a traceable and mandatory financial relationship with consumers. The defining characteristic of SSS resources is that there is a traceable and mandatory financial relationship between customers of a supplier or utility and the electricity and/or contractual instruments from deliverable generation resources (including both carbon-free and fossil resources) used to supply their load. The intent of the SSS designation is to ensure fair allocation of these resources among customers, such that nonparticipating customers are not harmed by the exercise of claim(s) for SSS resources by reporting companies." Examples include regulated-cost-recovery default service, "Competitive or regulated suppliers complying with government-mandated clean energy procurement programs through the procurement of certificates on behalf of the load they serve" (e.g., RPS, CES, nuclear-support policies, FIT), and publicly owned facilities under a public service obligation. "Suppliers should allocate SSS electricity supply and related EACs … on a pro rata load share basis. However, the GHG Protocol cannot require suppliers to allocate…" "A reporting entity may claim, up to its pro rata share, the electricity and associated attributes from SSS resources in the market-based method." — S53 pp.26–27.
- 2026 status: open — "Standard Supply Service (may/should/shall)" — S04 slide 40 item 9; workshop topic — S04 slide 43. ISO WG4 "reached a full consensus to remove the SSS criterion from the standard" — S04 slide 61; S03 p.7. AMI traceability subgroup: "Scope 2 Standard supply service (SSS) will not be useful to address the issue of the interaction between the new traceability proposal and the regulatory additionality requirement." — S32 slide 43.

### Residual mix
- 2015: "The mix of energy generation resources and associated attributes such as GHG emissions in a defined geographic boundary left after contractual instruments have been claimed/retired/canceled. The residual mix can provide an emission factor for companies without contractual instruments to use in a market-based method calculation." — S61 Glossary, PDF p.108 (printed p.106). [GHGP-inforce]

### "Actions" and "market instruments"
- "Action : Project, investment/financing, production/sale of products, purchase/consumption of products, or other intervention or activity that leads to changes in GHG emissions and removals (without regard to inventory boundary)." — S27 §5.1 p.16.
- "Market instruments: A contractual arrangement between two or more parties that enables the creation, transfer, or claiming of GHG-related environmental attributes." ### "Impact" / "consequential"
- "Consequential accounting: A type of GHG accounting that estimates the impacts or changes in GHG emissions (and removals) resulting from specific projects, actions, or interventions relative to a baseline scenario in which the project, action, or intervention did not occur. - "Attributional accounting (also known as inventory accounting): A type of GHG accounting that quantifies total GHG emissions (and removals and other accounting categories, if applicable) within a defined inventory boundary and time period." — S27 pp.17–18.

> **Scope 2 Market-based Method Update: From 2025-2026 Public Consultation to Current Status**
>
> Executive summary
> The purpose of this resource is to provide an update on progress of the scope 2 workstream following the release of the public consultation summary, subsequent Technical Working Group (TWG) and Independent Standard Board (ISB) inputs, and to further clarify the technical details of the approaches being discussed. This resource and all the processes referred to are in adherence with GHG Protocol's transparent, multi-stakeholder governance process.

Building on public consultation feedback and subsequent discussions, the workshop will bring together TWG members, ISB members, and the Secretariat to work together on the key issues, develop proposals, and ideally will result in a consensus draft on the main points. The initial framework, approved by the ISB, is a guide to these conversations. It brings together the learnings from the process thus far and intentionally leaves open the major questions to be debated and decided by the diverse experts in the TWG.
> The workshop is designed to provide an open environment for sharing of different perspectives and proposals to be considered. It is also designed to facilitate discussion and coordination between the TWG and ISB.
> Subsequent sections of the document outline technical information on the approaches that are being discussed and an outline of the steps that have been taken since the publication of the scope 2 summary consultation, as well as information on the intended next steps.
>
> 2025-2026 Scope 2 Public Consultation
> When the Scope 2 TWG was launched in September 2024 [https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/release-ghg-protocol-announces-technical-working-groups-update-process-suite-corporate], the group's task was to analyze the current Scope 2 Guidance (2015) and initial stakeholder feedback and proposals for revisions [https://ghgprotocol.org/survey-need-ghg-protocol-corporate-standards-and-guidance-updates] received in 2023 and then to develop proposed revisions for public consultation.
> The proposed revisions recommended by the Scope 2 TWG and approved by the Independent Standards Board [https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/scope-2-standard-advances-isb-approves-consultation-market-and-location-based-revisions] to progress to public consultation sought to address the challenges with the current scope 2 market-based method (2015) by requiring that organizations only report energy attribute certificates in the market-based method if they are:
> - matched to hourly electricity consumption (hourly matching), and
> - connected to deliverable grid regions where the organization consumes electricity (deliverability).
> The decision to propose new hourly matching and deliverability accounting and reporting requirements for the scope 2 market-based method was rooted in the growing need for more accurate emissions accounting. Under the current market-based method (from the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance) companies can report emissions reductions from clean energy projects located on grids that are disconnected from a company's operations. This disconnect means these projects are often not tied to the electric grid that supplies the company's operations and therefore not tied to its operations or value chain, reducing the accuracy and credibility of a company's reported emissions.
> For more information about hourly matching and deliverability please refer to this blog [https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/upcoming-scope-2-public-consultation-hourly-matching-and-deliverability] as well as the full scope 2 public consultation document [https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/GHG-Protocol-Scope2-Public-Consultation.pdf].
>
> Scope 2 Public Consultation Feedback
> The scope 2 public consultation ran from October 20, 2025 to January 31, 2026. The feedback on the scope 2 market-based method can be broadly categorized into two theories of change:
> - Theory of change 1: These stakeholders responded that accounting for clean electricity that is matched hourly, delivered on the same grid, and generated by plants built within a set number of years (often called incrementality) makes it more likely that corporate demand supports new clean power. By directing demand toward clean generation in the times and places it would otherwise be scarce, research has shown this approach reduces system-level emissions and drives grid decarbonization.
> - Theory of change 2: These stakeholders responded that allowing organizations to account for their electricity attribute purchases using annual matching and broad geographic boundaries minimizes barriers to market participation and supports a large, active market for energy attribute certificates (EACs), one that is easy to buy into regardless of location. This channels voluntary capital into the global renewable energy market with minimal constraints, which stakeholders noted could accelerate decarbonization.
> For more information about the feedback received during the scope 2 workstream's initial public consultation, please see the Scope 2 Summary of Feedback Executive Summary [https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/S2-ExecutiveSummary-PublicConsultation%20ummaryofFeedback-2026.07.29.pdf] and full scope 2 summary document [https://ghgprotocol.org/sites/default/files/2026-08/S2-PublicConsultationSummaryofFeedback-2026.08.25.pdf].
>
> Initial ISB Direction based on Public Consultation Feedback
> In their April 2026 meeting, the ISB clarified the intended sequencing of ISB and TWG engagement following the public consultation by advising that the ISB should set the initial direction for proposed revisions emerging from the scope 2 public consultation feedback. Then the Scope 2 TWG would be tasked with resolving unanswered questions and development of the approach for further consideration by the ISB.
> Following the ISB's directive, the Scope 2 Secretariat developed a proposed option for the ISB's review, proposing a role for two market-based methods that correspond with the two theories of change that emerged from the public consultation feedback (with open questions on whether either should be optional or required):
> - Method 1: This approach would incorporate hourly matching, deliverability and incrementality accounting and reporting requirements.
> - Method 2: This approach would maintain existing scope 2 requirements from the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance, allowing organizations to continue their current procurement strategies and addressing stakeholder concerns regarding feasibility and market participation.
> In the July 2026 ISB meeting, ISB members indicated majority support for the idea of developing two market-based methods with differentiated requirements and claims.
>
> Updated Direction of Travel based on ISB and Scope 2 TWG Feedback
> In July, the Scope 2 Secretariat shared the public consultation feedback and the initial direction of travel approved by the ISB with the Scope 2 TWG members. From late July to early September 2026, the Scope 2 TWG provided detailed feedback through a survey on the proposed dual market-based method framework.
> Based on the feedback from the Scope 2 TWG, the Scope 2 Secretariat provided the ISB with updated options for discussion in their September meeting.
> The three proposed options for implementing a dual market-based metric framework were:
> - two methods with different metrics (tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO2e) and another metric to be determined)
> - two methods both measured in tCO2e in perpetuity,
> - two methods both measured in tCO2e with a phased transition from method 2 to method 1 over time.
> After discussion of how each of the three options related to GHG Protocol's decision-making criteria (integrity, impact, and feasibility), the ISB proposed the following direction of travel to be further refined by the Scope 2 TWG:
> - Companies [shall/should/may, language still to be determined] report under the market-based method if data that meets the quality criteria is available. If a company does report scope 2 market-based method emissions:
> - Companies may report using Method 2 (current market-based method under 2015 Scope 2 Guidance) until a to-be-determined date,
> - Companies should report using Method 1 until a to-be-determined date,
> - Prior to that date, companies shall disclose whether they are applying market-based Method 1 or Method 2, and
> - After that date, if a company reports scope 2 market-based method emissions, companies shall report using Method 1 if data that meets the quality criteria is available.
>
> Connections with Actions and Market Instruments (AMI) Workstream
> In their September meeting, ISB members also discussed the important role of the multi-statement reporting framework developed by the Actions and Market Instruments (AMI) TWG in providing reporting pathways for electricity procurement activities that may not meet future market-based method requirements.
> Earlier this year, the AMI workstream held a Request for Information on their proposed multi-statement reporting framework [https://ghgprotocol.org/blog/announcement-ghg-protocol-releases-ami-feedback-summary-showing-broad-support-proposed-multi], which includes three new statements beyond the current physical GHG inventory:
> - Market-based GHG Inventory Statement - for reporting contractual arrangements, procurement choices, environmental attributes, and certificates,
> - GHG Impact Statement - for reporting the real-world emissions impacts of a company's actions and interventions, inside and outside its value chain,
> - Non-GHG Indicators Statement - for reporting key indicators, such as investment and procurement indicators, that provide insight into decarbonization efforts but are not reported as greenhouse gas emissions.
> The ISB noted in their feedback that electricity procurement activities and contractual instruments not reported in the market-based GHG inventory statement may be reported in the GHG impact statement or as key indicators.

### Q2. Who decides what — process map, Terms of Reference roles, decision-making criteria

**F2.1 The overarching process (SDRP §2, S39 p.4; graphic read visually).** "The development or revision of a standard shall include the following steps, with revisions undergoing these steps on a five-year cycle:" Preparation (definition of need, objective and scope; identification of steps and timeline) → Development/Revision process (working groups/consensus making and decision-making; consultation and alignment through ISB) → Public consultation (publication of proposal; public consultation, feedback analysis and summary; *if necessary:* recalibration and draft reissued, with summary of changes) → Approval ("Decision by ISB") → Ratification ("Decision by SC") → Publication ("On GHG Protocol website") → Post-implementation review / piloting ("Where applicable").

**F2.2 Step-by-step rules (SDRP, S39).**
- Need/scope: "The SC shall be the final arbiter of whether a new standard or a standard revision shall proceed." (§3.1.3, p.5). SDP: "Prior to commencement of the work, the SDP and workplan shall be approved by the ISB, as well as any subsequent amendments to either." (§3.2.5, p.5).
- TWGs: "The TWGs shall provide technical input to the Secretariat within their assigned scope, to support in developing proposals and recommendations to the ISB for the content of the applicable draft standard." (§4.1.2, p.5).
- General comments paused: "During the standard development or revision process, the collection of general comments or questions relating to a particular standard or subject matter area shall be paused – any comments or questions arising shall instead be submitted once the relevant Public Consultation period pursuant to Section 4.4 has commenced." (§4.3.4, p.6).
- Public consultation: "A formal public consultation shall be carried out for all draft new standards or major revisions during the standard development and revision process, consisting of at least one round of sixty (60) days for submission of comments by stakeholders, with the option for a longer initial duration and/or a further round of consultation upon approval by the ISB." (§4.4.1 as amended v1.2, p.6). Representativeness check: §4.4.4–4.4.5 (p.7). All feedback published with stakeholder type, sector, region (§4.5.1, p.7).
- Recalibration: "Based on the comments received during the public consultation period and as necessary, the Secretariat shall, together with the TWGs and ISB, review and revise the draft standard." (§4.6.1, p.7); then the Secretariat "shall: i. Submit the standard for approval to the ISB, or ii. Make a proposal to the ISB on any further consultation or pilot testing required before the standard is submitted for approval." (§4.6.2, p.7).
- Pilot testing: "If pilot testing has been approved by the ISB, the Secretariat shall i. Issue an open call for participation in pilot testing via the GHG Protocol website; ii. Create a list of proposed pilot testing participants, ensuring a range of industries and geographies are represented; iii. make a proposal for a final Pilot Testing Group (PTG) to the ISB for approval." (§4.7.2) and "A pilot testing period shall last at least sixty (60) days" (§4.7.4) — p.8.
- Publication: "Where a final version draft standard has been approved by the ISB following receipt from the Secretariat, the ISB shall present this for ratification to the SC pursuant to the applicable ISB ToR." (§4.8.1, p.8); the report must summarize "the main issues of contention and concerns raised by TWG members during the process and how the Secretariat has responded to these" (§4.8.2 ii). "Following ratification, the standard shall be published on the GHG Protocol website by the Co-Hosts" (§4.8.3, p.8).
- Departures: "Departures from the procedures contained within this document may be approved by the ISB in exceptional circumstances…" (§5.1.3, p.9).

**F2.6 Decision-making criteria and hierarchy (verbatim; Governance Overview Annex A, v1.0 24.09.2024, S40 pp.6–9).**
- Purpose: "The purpose of the decision-making criteria is to support the GHG Protocol Secretariat, Technical Working Groups, and Independent Standards Board in evaluating GHG Protocol accounting and reporting approaches to determine which option among a defined set of options best adheres to the criteria and should be pursued. The decision-making criteria in aggregate are intended to support the GHG Protocol mission and vision."
- Hierarchy: "The criteria are ranked in a hierarchy to aid in decision making. When options present tradeoffs between criteria, the hierarchy should be generally followed, such that, for example, scientific integrity is not compromised at the expense of other criteria, while aiming to find solutions that meet all criteria."
- Summary version: "1. Integrity — Science and principles"; "2. Impact — Support ambitious global climate action and programs"; "3. Feasibility to implement".
- Full version:
  1. **1A. Scientific integrity** — "First, approaches should ensure scientific integrity and validity, adhere to the best applicable science and evidence (including academic literature, modeling or other research) and align with the latest climate science."
  2. **1B. GHG accounting and reporting principles** — "Second, approaches should meet the GHG Protocol accounting and reporting principles of accuracy, completeness, consistency, relevance, and transparency. Additional principles should be considered where relevant: conservativeness (for GHG reductions and removals), permanence (for removals), and comparability (TBD, subject to TWG and ISB discussions). Options may present tradeoffs among principles which should be evaluated. Refer to section A.3 for further details."
  3. **2A. Support decision making that drives ambitious global climate action** — "Third, approaches should advance the public interest by informing and supporting decision making that drives ambitious actions by private and public sector actors to reduce GHG emissions and increase removals in line with global climate goals." "GHG Protocol accounting frameworks should accurately and completely measure emissions such that the resulting GHG data informs effective individual and systemwide GHG mitigation action in line with global climate goals. Accounting approaches should not support or incentivize actions that are contrary to global climate goals." "Approaches should provide the necessary information to support sector-specific decarbonization in line with climate goals."
  4. **2B. Support programs based on GHG Protocol and uses of GHG data** — "Fourth, approaches should promote interoperability with key mandatory and voluntary climate disclosure and target setting programs that are based on GHG Protocol standards, where appropriate, while ensuring policy neutrality. Refer to section A.4 for further details." "Approaches should support appropriate uses of the resulting GHG data and associated information by various audiences, including GHG programs, reporting companies, stakeholders and other users of the resulting GHG information."
  5. **3. Feasibility to implement** — "Fifth, approaches which meet the above criteria should be feasible to implement, meaning that they are accessible, adoptable, and equitable." "GHG Protocol accounting approaches should support broad adoption of GHG Protocol standards, including in voluntary and regulatory settings, and consider different users (level of capacity, resources, geography, regulatory environments, etc.)." "For aspects of accounting approaches that meet the above criteria but are difficult to implement, the GHG Protocol should aim to improve feasibility, for example, by providing guidance and tools to support implementation."
- A.3 principle definitions (working version) include: "A. Accuracy: Ensure that the quantification of GHG emissions (and removals, if applicable) is systematically neither over nor under actual emissions (and removals, if applicable), and that uncertainties are reduced as far as practicable…" and "H. Comparability (TBD, subject to TWG and ISB discussion)…" (p.8–9).
- A.4 policy neutrality: "GHG Protocol standards are policy relevant and intended to support the larger objective of reducing GHG emissions and increasing removals in line with global climate goals, but the standard is not designed to favor one policy mechanism over another." (p.9).
- Approval history: ISB #2 (Sep 11 2024) "ISB voted unanimously to approve the decision-making criteria" (S22); SC 2024/Q3 (Sep 24 2024) "unanimously and provisionally approved… with the proviso that (i) minor clarification wording edits will be made… and (ii) that the Decision-Making Criteria will be revisited in due course as necessary." (S26). The Consolidated SDP: "all governance and advisory bodies of the GHG Protocol shall follow the decision-making criteria and hierarchy approved by the Independent Standards Board and Steering Committee" (S38 p.12).
- Note: the All-TWG kickoff deck (Sep 30 2024, S62) does not contain the criteria text; it lists "Finalization of Decision-making Criteria…" as a next step (slide 38).

### Q5. How a stakeholder (developer, generator, competitive retail supplier) can shape the standard from here

Formal channels, with status as of 2026-10-19:
1. **Consolidated public consultation, Q2 2027** — "All interested stakeholders have the opportunity to review the draft revised standard during the consolidated public consultation phase." (S38 p.17). Minimum 60 days (S39 §4.4.1). "ISB determines whether any Scope 2 topics require a second round of public consultation at that time." (S38 p.16). Outside consultation windows, general comments "shall be paused" (S39 §4.3.4).
2. **ISO route (INFERENCE)** — the Q2 2027 draft also goes to "ISO/TC 207/SC 7 … through its committee draft (CD) stage, wherein the draft is circulated for committee-level comments" (S38 p.16); ISO approval is by national-body vote (S38 p.16; S04 slide 48). Sources do not describe how an individual company engages; INFERENCE: via its national standards body.
3. **TWG membership** — appointed by the ISB on Secretariat recommendation; "the ISB may at any time appoint further TWG members, should a significant representation gap, specific area of expertise or other need be identified." (S43 §4.3.4). The rolling-application form "will be closed" after Jan 15 2025 (S63). GAP: no currently open TWG call found.
4. **Engaging TWG members/ISB** — whether TWG members may engage ISB members directly was pending ("it will consult the ISB Chair on member engagement and revert" — S03 p.6). TWG members represent themselves: "should not advocate on behalf of external consortia or associations within a TWG" (S43 §4.1.5).
5. **ISB membership** — call for applications closed "11:59 PM ET August 8, 2026" (S64); SC to see "at least 3 candidates… in its September meeting" (S23 p.2).
6. **Complaints and concerns** — any time, "via the form available on the GHG Protocol website at www.ghgprotocol.org/contact-us" (S44 §3.1.2). Complaints "with merit" must allege due-process deviation "that can be substantiated with direct evidence" (e.g. "Deviation from established decision-making criteria") or identify "a factual error, outdated scientific assumption, or methodological flaw that can be substantiated with peer-reviewed literature or authoritative sources" (S44 §2.2, p.5). Due-process complaints go to an ISB subcommittee; scientific/technical ones are investigated by the Secretariat with "Any corrective action… according to the Standards Development and Revision Procedure" (S44 §4.1–4.2). Target "20 working days" with possible extension (S44 §4.5.1). Responses to complaints with merit are published (§4.5.2). Track record: consultation extension complaints → SC extended (S48, S25); AMI RFI complaints → extension to June 15 (S48); minutes publication complaint → ISB minutes 1–22 published by July 24 2026 (S46).
7. **Pilot/road testing** — only if the ISB approves (S39 §4.7.2; open call). AMI: RFI asked respondents about "participating in future pilot testing" (S11 p.2); Sept 2026: "whether a formalized public-facing pilot process can be run in parallel with the public consultation and guidance development in 2027 can be evaluated" (S31 p.3).
8. **Interpretation/clarification procedures** after publication (S39 §7: interpretations need ≥30-day consultation, ISB approval, SC ratification).
9. **Subscribe** for updates (S01; S38 p.17).

**WattTime complaint (S45) — what it argues and what procedure it invokes.**

Thesis: "The Greenhouse Gas Protocol's (GHG Protocol) proposed Scope 2 update to the market-based method (MBM) introduces requirements for deliverable and time-matched energy but excludes the principle of additionality… This has been justified almost exclusively on the basis of studies that analyze the joint effect of deliverability, hourly-matched energy, and additionality constraints, collectively often referred to as the 'Three Pillars' or sometimes '24/7.' … While we, the authors of this complaint, take no position on whether the GHG Protocol should adopt the Three Pillars, using studies of all three to justify adopting only two undermines the Protocol's stated decision-making criteria of integrity and impact."
