Can a CCS plant’s power be sold as a low-carbon attribute?

The 2015 Scope 2 Guidance does not define it, and on this page’s reading (an inference) nothing in it bars it. This page traces what a certificate can carry, the unresolved emission-rate question, how the eight Quality Criteria apply, where the revision is heading, and which third-party instruments exist today.

1 · 2015 Guidance

What an EAC can represent

The 2015 Guidance treats certificates as a carrier of information about any generation, not as a renewable-only product.

“Historically, most certificates for policies or consumer programs have been generated from renewable energy resources, driven by demand for these resources in particular, but depending on their intended purpose or usage certificates can be generated from any or all generation technology types. For example, all-generation certificate tracking exists in the northeast U.S.”

“All energy generation has a GHG emission rate attribute, even if that attribute is 'zero emissions/MWh' at the point of generation.”

Scope 2 Guidance (2015), §10.2, p.80 · source

“All energy has a direct emissions factor associated with generation, and the use of that emissions factor does not depend on whether the generation facility is existing or new, or why the generation has occurred.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015, p.90

The market-based method is meant to include fossil instruments too: “the market-based method should also include other contractual instruments representing fossil fuel or mixed-resource emission factors as well.” Table 6.3 lists “In the U.S., contracts for electricity from specified nonrenewable sources like coal in regions other than NEPOOL and PJM”, and a footnote explains that “Only the NEPOOL and PJM regions of the U.S. use all generation certificate tracking.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015 §7.4.2 p.63; Table 6.3 p.48; p.31

Attribute
“Descriptive or performance characteristics of a particular generation resource. For scope 2 GHG accounting, the GHG emission rate attribute of the energy generation is required to be included in a contractual instrument in order to make a claim.”
Scope 2 Guidance 2015 glossary · p.99
Renewable energy certificate (REC)
“A type of energy attribute certificate, used in the U.S. and Australia. In the U.S., a REC is defined as representing the property rights to the generation, environmental, social, and other non-power attributes of renewable electricity generation.”
Scope 2 Guidance 2015 glossary · p.106
Gap · no eligibility list

The 2015 Guidance sets no technology eligibility list and no “low-carbon” class. It uses “low-carbon” only descriptively, and calls its criteria neutral: “These Scope 2 Quality Criteria are policy-neutral”. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, p.8, Box 1.1 p.9

Inference

Nothing in the 2015 text bars an EAC from a CCS-equipped fossil plant. It would be an instrument with a non-zero emission rate. Because a REC is renewable-only by definition, it would be a generic EAC or a fossil-source contract, not a REC. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, pp.80, 106

2 · Emission rate

The emission factor problem

The certificate must carry the plant’s “direct” rate. For a plant that captures part of its CO2, no GHG Protocol text says which number that is.

Quality Criterion 1: the instrument shall “Convey the direct GHG emission rate attribute associated with the unit of electricity produced.” Table 6.3 adds: “Data forms listed here should convey combustion-only (direct) GHG emission rates, expressed in metric tons per MWh or kWh.” Section 6.11: “To ensure this, instruments shall include the GHG emission rate attribute.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015 Table 7.1 p.60; Table 6.3 p.48; §6.11 p.54

The rate is generation-only. Emission factors “do not include T&D losses or upstream life-cycle emissions associated with the technology or fuel used in generation. Instead, these other categories of upstream emissions should be quantified and reported in scope 3, category 3”. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 §4.2, p.27

Where the certificate carries no rate: “If the attribute emission rate itself is not specified and the technology is not zero emissions, the reporting organization should seek from the generating entity a specific emission rate from that generation facility. Otherwise, a default factor from IPCC or other government publications may be used and disclosed.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015 §7.5, p.64

Gap · gross or post-capture

The 2015 text does not say whether a CCS plant’s “direct” or “combustion-only” rate means gross combustion CO2 or CO2 emitted after capture. A search of the 2015 Guidance for “captur”, “CCS”, “sequest” and “carbon storage” found no carbon-capture text. Scope 2 Guidance 2015

Inference · the post-capture reading

The Corporate Standard defines emissions as releases, and LSR excludes captured and stored CO2 from the generator’s scope 1 and the buyer’s scope 2. A “direct” rate consistent with LSR would therefore be the post-capture emitted rate: uncaptured stack CO2 plus capture-site fugitive emissions, per MWh. It holds only while the LSR geologic-storage requirements are met: monitoring, site data, reversals. If a certificate carries no rate, the §7.5 default “may be used” would presumably mean an unabated fossil factor. LSR Guidance pp.359–363 Scope 2 Guidance 2015 p.64

The 2015 text has a close analogue for a plant whose CO2 is treated differently: “Biofuel generation facilities producing certificates should specify the CO2, CH4, and N2O emissions produced at the point of generation. The scope 2 reporter reports the CH4 and N2O emissions in scope 2, while the CO2 from biofuel is reported separately from the scopes.” Scope 2 Guidance 2015, p.64

Gap · gross or net MWh

The 2015 Guidance is silent on whether a CCS certificate is issued per gross MWh or per net MWh after the parasitic load of capture and compression. EnergyTag’s standard answers for its own scheme: “Auxiliary energy needed to produce the output energy shall be deducted from the output when determining the qualifying output for the Issuance of GCs.” The NorthBridge proposal divides by Net MWh. EnergyTag GC Standard v2, p.36 NorthBridge CCS EAC Standard, Eq. 1 p.30

3 · Quality Criteria

The eight Quality Criteria, one by one

Each criterion below is quoted from Table 7.1, then what the 2015 text says around it, then how it would apply to a certificate from a CCS-equipped plant. Every application is an inference: the GHG Protocol has not assessed CCS certificates.

Inference · double claiming the same tonnes

A CCS plant can generate two products from one physical fact: carbon credits for stored tonnes, and certificates whose low rate exists because those tonnes were stored. Selling both lets two buyers claim the same tonnes. The 2015 Guidance’s offsets check (Table 8.1) and its rule that a MWh cannot produce both a certificate and an offset credit point toward choosing one. The scope of the revision flags only “interactions between carbon credits sourced from carbon-free generation facilities and EACs from the same resource”, which does not name CCS. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 pp.68, 72 Standard Development Plan, pp.22–23

Disclosure applies whatever the instrument: “For the market-based method, companies shall disclose the category or categories of instruments from which the emission factors were derived, where possible specifying the energy generation technologies.” Table 8.1 recommends disclosing “Energy resource type”, “Facility location” and “Facility age … the year the generation facility that created the certificate/contract was first operational or substantially repowered”. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 §7.1 p.61; Table 8.1 p.68

Gap · retrofit and facility age

“Substantially repowered” is not defined. Whether a CCS retrofit counts, and so resets the facility age a buyer discloses, is not addressed. Scope 2 Guidance 2015, Table 8.1 p.68

4 · Revision

The revision: no technology rules, a proposed age limit

The 2025 draft changed how and where certificates match load, not which plants can issue them. The direction since July 2026 adds an age test that a CCS retrofit may or may not pass.

The 2025 consultation draft

The draft’s market-based changes, open for consultation 20 Oct 2025 to 31 Jan 2026, were six. It proposed to “Update the Scope 2 Quality Criteria 4 to require that all contractual instruments used in the market-based method be issued and redeemed for the same hour as the energy consumption to which the instrument is applied, except in certain cases of exemption”; to update Quality Criteria 5 so instruments “otherwise meet criteria deemed to demonstrate deliverability”; plus a Standard Supply Service share, a new residual mix definition, a fossil-based fallback factor, and feasibility measures. No item addresses technology eligibility or how the rate on instruments is derived. Scope 2 consultation draft §5.1, pp.5, 19–20

The fallback: “In cases where a residual mix is not available, reporting organizations shall use a fossil-based emission factor that reflects electricity generation within the relevant market boundary.” “Generic location-based average grid emission factors shall not be used.” Scope 2 consultation draft, p.28

Gap · no CCS anywhere in the revision record

A full-text search of the draft for “captur”, “CCS”, “sequest”, “low-carbon” and related terms found no CCS hits. The Summary of Feedback has none for “carbon capture”, “CCS” or “sequestration”. Across all 82 Scope 2 PDFs in the governance repository (Sept 2024 to Oct 2026), searches for “carbon capture”, “CCS”, “CCUS”, “sequestration” and “capture and stor” returned zero hits in every file. The draft also does not say how CCS-equipped fossil generation would enter the “fossil-based” fallback factor. Consultation draft Summary of Feedback Governance repository

Inference · CCS under the draft

A CCS certificate would face the same hourly and deliverability tests as any other, and Quality Criterion 1 would still require the direct emission rate. Hourly certificates would need per-hour rates if capture performance varies; the draft does not discuss this. Scope 2 consultation draft, pp.20–23

Incrementality was not in the draft. Respondents “noted that the proposed market-based method revisions do not include an incrementality requirement…”. Summary of Feedback v1.0, p.55

Since July 2026: two methods

The October update describes a “Theory of change 1” as “accounting for clean electricity that is matched hourly, delivered on the same grid, and generated by plants built within a set number of years (often called incrementality)”. Method 1 “would incorporate hourly matching, deliverability and incrementality accounting and reporting requirements”; Method 2 “would maintain existing scope 2 requirements from the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance”. The ISB direction: “Companies may report using Method 2 … until a to-be-determined date”, after which, “if a company reports scope 2 market-based method emissions, companies shall report using Method 1 if data that meets the quality criteria is available”. This is a direction of travel to be refined by the TWG, not a decision. Whether companies report market-based emissions at all is still bracketed “[shall/should/may, language still to be determined]”. Scope 2 MBM Update, Oct 2026, pp.1–2

The incrementality row, “as presented to the September ISB”, reads “Age limit on eligible resources for voluntary claims (shall)” for Method 1 and “No requirement” for Method 2. The design details “could include: Qualifying operational events within [X] yrs (e.g., new build, repowering, clean conversion, upgrade, or life extension); original off-taker; pro-rata grid-share allocation; qualifying storage”. TWG #23 deck, pp.34, 40

Inference · retrofit

“Clean conversion” or “upgrade” could in principle cover a CCS retrofit, comparable to 45V’s CCS retrofit rule. The deck does not say so. Gap TWG #23 deck, p.40

Signal of direction · 6 Oct 2026

TWG members “raised concerns about the inclusion of incrementality in Method 1, suggesting … that it is relevant to assessing impact but not necessary for allocating emissions attributes to consumption”. The Secretariat said “the ISB supported its inclusion on integrity and impact grounds, that it is open for development by the TWG”. TWG #23 minutes, p.6

Signal of direction · ISO divergence, 6 Oct 2026

The ISO working group (WG4) “proposed to use the grid average mix as the fallback”, “reached full consensus to remove the Standard Supply Service criterion”, set “monthly matching as the mandatory minimum … hourly matching optional”, and reported that “deliverability was removed as not practical”. Reconciliation with the ISB direction is pending. TWG #23 minutes, pp.6–7

The NorthBridge proposal in detail

“One EAC is issued for each megawatt-hour of generation. The EAC is minted with an associated emission rate measured in pounds of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) per megawatt-hour. When retired, the attribute holder may claim the pounds of CO2e listed on the attribute in its market-based Scope 2 inventory.” The rate “includes emissions from electricity generation, CO2 capture, CO2 transport, and CO2 storage”. NorthBridge CCS EAC Standard, pp.5–6

Inference · boundary conflict

The LSR Guidance already assigns transport, storage and off-site fugitive emissions to the buyer’s scope 3 category 3, not scope 2. A single all-in scope 2 rate, as NorthBridge proposes, would not match LSR once it takes effect on 01 Jan 2027. LSR Guidance pp.359–360

On double counting the proposal states: “Registering Entities may not pursue carbon credits (or other environmental attributes) for the emissions captured and stored within the project boundary that are included in the calculation methodology and reflected in an EAC.” On tax credits: “Pursuing federal tax credits and/or avoiding state carbon taxes or penalties in addition to generating EACs under this methodology is not considered double counting.” It sets no additionality test: “This standard does not require imposing additionality requirements on projects that apply for EACs.” NorthBridge pp.18–19, 45 A trade-press op-ed by the authors describes it as a standard for registries. Utility Dive, 12 Dec 2025

6 · Position

What to take into a position

For a developer or generator weighing CCS-backed low-carbon power as a market product.

  • SourcedThe 2015 Guidance lets certificates come “from any or all generation technology types” and expects fossil and mixed-resource instruments. There is no eligibility list. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 pp.63, 80 Inference A CCS certificate would be a non-REC instrument with a non-zero rate.
  • SourcedThe instrument must convey the “direct” emission rate, “combustion-only (direct)”; the GHG Protocol has never said whether that means gross or post-capture CO2. Scope 2 Guidance 2015 pp.48, 60
  • InferenceThe defensible rate is the post-capture emitted rate per MWh, valid only while the LSR storage requirements are met. Transport and storage emissions belong in the buyer’s scope 3 category 3, so an all-in scope 2 rate would not match LSR.
  • InferenceThe two integrity risks to design out are criterion 2 (carbon credits for the same tonnes) and criterion 8 (residual mix that still contains the plant’s low rate).
  • SignalThe revision’s Method 1 would add an “Age limit on eligible resources for voluntary claims (shall)”, with “clean conversion, upgrade” on a draft design list, plus hourly matching and deliverability. None of it is adopted. TWG #23 deck pp.34, 40
  • GapNo GHG Protocol position on CCS power certificates; no rule on gross versus net MWh; no answer on whether a retrofit is “substantially repowered” or a “clean conversion”; no mechanism for an unbundled buyer to meet LSR’s monitoring duty.
Takeaway

Today a CCS-backed certificate is permitted by silence, not by design. The 2015 rules would carry it as a non-zero-rate instrument, but the GHG Protocol has defined neither its emission rate nor its fit with the revision’s proposed age test, and a product built now should be able to adapt to either answer.
