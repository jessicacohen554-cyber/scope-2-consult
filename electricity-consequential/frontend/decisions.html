The Decision Board

Every closed decision point of the consultation, in one visual grammar

This survey asked for very few closed answers, and they are scattered across five sections of a twenty-page consultation document. The board pulls all of them onto one page: what was asked, who answered it, and how much of the room was still there when the question came up. Every panel is drawn by the same renderers, so a strip means the same thing wherever you meet it — and every share carries the base it was divided by.

Where the room stands

Questions that carry a polarity in the dataset are painted with the support ramp and the critical answer in red; the rest get a neutral ramp, because a preference is not a verdict. Non-substantive answers stay gray and are never netted. Activate any bar for its full counts, its segment split and a link to the topic page that unpacks it.

The additionality gradient

Each additionality test rated required, optional or not required, sorted by net requiredness. This is an ordered stringency ladder and not a sentiment scale, so it is painted with the sequential ramp the design system reserves for ordered constructs — nothing here is agreement or disagreement.

The methodology scoreboards

Each family of methods was asked twice — which are appropriate, and which are not — as two separate multi-selects answered by two different groups of people. Not-appropriate runs left, appropriate runs right, and each side is a share of its own base, both of which are printed on every axis. The specials sit beside each board and are never folded into the net.

How much evidence sits under each panel

Responses per substantive question, in survey order. Attrition here is steep and close to monotone: by the end of the questionnaire most of the room has gone. That is the reason every panel on this site divides by the base of its own question and prints it — a bare percentage taken from one end of this strip and compared with one from the other end is a misreading, not a finding.
