02The headline: the proposed formula does not command consent

— of the — who answered call the Scope 2 TWG subgroup formula inappropriate (— No against — Yes). That is the consultation's single clearest signal, and it is a rejection of the formula as drafted, not of consequential accounting.

The No side is not one argument. Read against the coded explanations on the formula page, its largest component is incompleteness — the objection that the formula does not net induced emissions against avoided ones — followed by a distinct group arguing venue and sequence rather than substance: that this belongs with the Scope 2 workstream, in that order. The theme coding is multi-label and the groups overlap, so they are emphases, not a partition, and the counts on that page are per-question totals rather than a breakdown of the No side.

07Interest, coordination and integrity — symmetric skepticism

Interest runs in both directions here. Modeling consultancies and data vendors gain from complex, granular methods; reporting companies gain from simple, coarse ones. Both interests are present in this record and neither invalidates an argument — it only means no participant's answer should be read as disinterested.

Coordination is measurable. — blocks of identical text of 200 characters or more are shared across — respondents, forming — response packs. The largest is —. Which country is not named here or anywhere on this site, and is not recoverable from anything published on it: naming it would be new disclosure about people who asked not to be identified. Deduplication matters to the headlines: counting shared text once moves Q19's No count to — and Q21's to —.

Redaction is not evenly spread, and that is a confound worth stating plainly. Companies are the only organization type where withholding identity is the majority — — redacted against — named, of —. Since companies are also the largest type in the room, any sentence of the form “redacted respondents think X” is substantially “companies think X” and has to be hedged that way.

Redacted is not a verdict. Withholding a name is an ordinary and permitted choice in a public consultation. Redacted respondents are counted in every aggregate on this site and are never named anywhere. Where their answers differ from named respondents', it is reported as a measured difference with its n and its significance, never as a motive.

08How a third party should read this

The signal is strongest on four things: the formula as proposed does not have consent; the regulatory and timing tests are the ones this room wants required; annual reporting is settled, most firmly among US respondents; and average-emission-rate build margin is clearly rejected. Those are the claims the record supports.

Genuinely open: whether secondary effects belong in scope, both granularity questions, and most of the methodology middle. On those, the honest summary is that this consultation did not resolve them, and a document that claims otherwise is over-reading it.

What would settle them is not another consultation. It is published back-tests of the competing marginal-rate methods, registry pilots that show the additionality tests being applied at cost, and the kind of documentation that Q52 asked for and largely did not receive. Read this hub as evidence of where a well-informed, self-selected and US-weighted group of practitioners currently stands — which is a legitimate and useful thing for a standard-setter to know, and a different thing from a mandate. The methodology page documents every construct, base and caveat behind the numbers above.
