How they were found

Detection is mechanical and runs over all 185 rows, not over a shortlist. A respondent is flagged when at least half of their free-text answers are gibberish, where an answer counts as gibberish if it is under 20 characters and either has a vowel ratio below 0.30, is a single repeated character, or is a keyboard run.

  • The rule is applied to the whole export, so a redacted submission is caught on the same terms as a named one.
  • Every flag was then reviewed one at a time against the full submission before exclusion, and the reasoning was written down in reference/exclusions.csv.
  • A redacted submission is adjudicated on answer content alone. No attempt is made to infer who filed it.
  • A short answer is not a junk answer. A one-word reply to a question about a technical preference is a legitimate response, and the threshold is set to leave those alone.

What exclusion means here

An excluded submission is left out of every aggregate, every quote pool and the organization browser. It is not deleted, not hidden, and not characterized. These are data-quality records: a submission that carries no content cannot inform a percentage, and counting it would understate the strength of every result it dilutes.

The count matters more than it looks. At an analytical base of 180, five rows are 2.7% of the file — enough to move a close split, which is why the ledger is stated at the top of this page rather than buried in a methodology note.

What this page can and cannot claim

  • Redacted is not hiding. Withholding a name was an option the consultation offered and roughly two respondents in five took it. Redacted submissions are counted in every total on this site, are quoted where quoting is safe, and are never named. Nothing on this page infers who they are.
  • Shared text is not invalid text. Organizing a common position and filing it through several members is normal, legal consultation behaviour, and a coordinated argument can be entirely correct. The only claim made here is arithmetic: when one argument arrives five times, a count of respondents and a count of positions are different measurements.
  • Exclusion is mechanical and disclosed. The junk rule is a published threshold applied to all 185 rows, each flag was reviewed against the full submission, and the reasoning is in the repository. The excluded rows are printed above so the rulings can be checked and disputed.
  • The cross-consultation join sees only half the room. It matches organization names, so it can only find respondents who were named in both consultations. Redacted respondents on either side are invisible to it, which means the true overlap is larger than the table and cannot be measured from the record.
  • Citation counts measure form, not quality. Naming a source is not the same as being right, and an argument made from professional experience without a footnote is not thereby weaker. The rates above describe how the record is written, not who should be believed.
  • 185 self-selected respondents are not a sample. Nobody was drawn at random; people who cared enough to answer, answered, and more than half of them are from one country. Every percentage on this site describes the people who responded, and a consultation is not a referendum.
