Themes & voices

The free-text record — what respondents argued, in their own words, and how often

This consultation was argued in prose. It offered almost no reason picklists, so unlike a survey with tidy “why?” options, the arguments here have to be read before they can be counted. Every substantive free-text answer was read once and tagged against a closed vocabulary of themes; this page is that coded record, the quotes behind it, and the places where the same text was filed more than once.

The coded record

Every theme in the taxonomy with its total mentions, grouped by what the theme is — a concern, a design ask, a statement of support, or context. Polarity is a property of the theme, not a verdict on the respondent: a submission can raise four concerns and still support the direction of travel.

Two bars, two questions. The outer bar counts mentions: one per answer that argues the theme, so a respondent who makes the same argument under four questions is counted four times, and the mentions across a group add up to more than the number of answers. The inner bar counts the same mentions after collapsing each block of identical, circulated text to a single voice. Where the two bars diverge, the theme is travelling on a response pack rather than spreading on its own — the packs themselves are below.

Theme profile of a single question

The overview above pools questions. This one does not: pick a question and the shares are of that question’s own base, which is the only base a percentage on this site is ever taken of.

What they actually said

Every curated quote, filterable five ways. Quotes are verbatim substrings of the submitted answer, trimmed only with […] elisions. Redacted respondents appear by organization type and country and are never named; junk and superseded submissions are not quoted at all. A card badged template cluster is text this respondent shares verbatim with others — read it as one argument filed several times.

The response packs

Groups of respondents who filed the same passages, reconstructed from the shared-text clusters in the record and shown here as documents. Named members are listed and linked; members who requested redaction are counted and never named.

Who wrote how much

Free-text volume per respondent, in log-scale bands — because the distribution is log-scale. A handful of filers wrote more than the rest of the room combined, and every “respondents said” claim about this consultation is weighted by that fact whether it admits to it or not.
